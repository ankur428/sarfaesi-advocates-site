Borrowers often ask whether a bank must choose between the SARFAESI Act 2002 and the Recovery of Debts and Bankruptcy Act 1993 (the RDB Act). The Supreme Court's decision in *Transcore v. Union of India* (2008) 1 SCC 125 is commonly cited for a simple idea: **the two laws operate together as parts of one recovery framework.**

## Two routes, one purpose

Both laws exist to help banks and financial institutions recover dues from defaulting borrowers more quickly than an ordinary civil suit would allow.

- **The RDB Act** allows a bank to file an application before the Debts Recovery Tribunal (DRT) to recover its dues.
- **SARFAESI** allows a secured creditor to enforce its security interest directly, after following the required notice steps, without first going to a tribunal.

*Transcore* explained that these are not rival schemes. They are complementary remedies, and the Court read them together rather than treating one as cancelling the other.

## What this means for a borrower

In practical terms, a borrower should not assume that a bank's use of one law shuts out the other. A bank may have both routes available, and the way they fit together depends on the facts of the loan and the stage of the proceedings. The exact interaction, including what happens when proceedings under both laws are pending, should be checked against the current text of the Acts and the judgment itself.

It also helps to remember how the two laws connect on the borrower's side. If a borrower is unhappy with a step taken under SARFAESI, the remedy is generally an application before the DRT under Section 17, and a further appeal lies as the Act provides. So the DRT is relevant to both laws, which is part of why the Court treated them as working in tandem.

## Points to keep in mind

- Do not read a notice under one Act as meaning the other Act cannot be used.
- Keep every notice, order and account statement, as they show which route the bank has chosen and when.
- Note the time limits for any challenge. Check the current text of the Act for the period that applies to your situation.
- Seek advice early, because the choice of forum and the timing can affect your options.

## A word of caution

*Transcore* is one decision on a specific set of questions, and it should not be stretched beyond what it decided. Each recovery matter turns on its own documents and dates. This article is general information, not advice on any particular dispute.
