The DRAT appeal limitation of 30 days under Section 18 of the SARFAESI Act 2002 runs from the date the appellant receives the DRT order, not from the date the order was passed. In practice, that makes the date and proof of receipt the first facts to fix. Check the current text of the Act and the Tribunal's rules before relying on any detail here.

## What does the DRAT appeal limitation of 30 days mean?

Section 18 allows a person aggrieved by an order of the Debts Recovery Tribunal under Section 17 to appeal to the Appellate Tribunal. The appeal has to be filed within thirty days from the date of receipt of the order. The period is short, and it is separate from the pre-deposit that the appeal also requires.

Because the period is tied to receipt, two parties to the same case can have different starting dates. A borrower who was served later than the bank may count from a later day, and the record should show why.

## How is the period counted from receipt?

The usual approach is to note the date on which the certified copy or the order itself reached the appellant, and count thirty days from there. A few practical points help:

- Note whether the order was received in court, by post, by courier or by email, and keep the proof.
- If the order was received by an advocate, record the date the advocate got it and the date it was passed on.
- Ask what the rules say about excluding the first day when counting. Check the current text before relying on a calendar.
- Where the last day falls on a holiday or the Tribunal is closed, confirm the position instead of assuming extra time.

## Does applying for a certified copy change the date?

Appellants often wait for a certified copy before filing. Whether the time taken to obtain a copy is excluded from the thirty days is a question of the law and the Tribunal's procedure, and it should not be assumed. The safer course is to apply for the copy at once, note the date of application and the date of delivery, and file within the period wherever possible.

Our article on [pre-deposit under Section 18](/blog/2026-10-03-section-18-appeal-pre-deposit) explains why the deposit has to be arranged in parallel, since an appeal that is ready but not accompanied by the deposit may not be entertained.

## What if the thirty days have passed?

The Act fixes the period, and a late appeal asks the Tribunal to look at delay. Whether delay can be excused, and on what reasons, depends on the law and on the facts, and no outcome should be expected. A written explanation, with dates and papers, is more useful than a general statement of difficulty.

It is better to treat the thirty days as firm. ## Frequently asked questions

### Does the thirty days run from the date of the DRT order or from receipt?
Section 18 refers to the date of receipt of the order. Keep proof of the day it was received, because the starting point may be questioned.

### Can I wait for the certified copy before counting the thirty days?
Do not assume so. Whether the time spent obtaining a copy is excluded depends on the law and the Tribunal's procedure, so apply early and check the current position.

### Is the pre-deposit due on the last day of the thirty days?
The deposit is a condition of the appeal being entertained, so it should be planned alongside the filing. Check the current rules for how and when it is to be made.
