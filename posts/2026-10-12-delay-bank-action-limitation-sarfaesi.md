Many borrowers ask whether a bank can still act under the SARFAESI Act 2002 many years after a loan was classified as non-performing. The answer involves the law of limitation, which depends on the facts and is not always simple.

## The basic idea of limitation

Limitation laws set time limits within which a claim must be pursued. A debt that is too old may be unenforceable by a court or tribunal. The Limitation Act 1963 contains the general rules, including those on **acknowledgement of debt**, which can extend the time. **Check the current text of the Limitation Act** and the case law for the position on your facts.

## How this relates to SARFAESI

SARFAESI allows a secured creditor to enforce security without going to court. Whether, and how, limitation applies to such enforcement has been argued in many cases, and the answer can depend on the type of claim and the stage of the proceedings. Because the position is not the same in every situation, it should not be assumed that either delay or promptness settles the matter.

## Facts that often matter

1. **Date of default** and date of NPA classification.
2. **Date of the Section 13(2) notice** and whether earlier notices were sent.
3. **Any acknowledgement of the debt** in writing by the borrower, for example in a settlement letter or balance confirmation.
4. **Part payments** and when they were made.
5. **Earlier proceedings**, such as a recovery application before the Tribunal.
6. **Gaps in action** by the bank, and the reasons for them.

## Limits that do not wait

Whatever the position on the bank's delay, a borrower's own remedies have **strict time limits**. A Section 17 application before the Debts Recovery Tribunal must be filed within 45 days from the date on which the measure was taken, subject to what the Act provides. A borrower who waits, hoping the bank's claim is time-barred, may lose the right to challenge a specific step.

## What to do

- Reply to the notice in writing and record any limitation objection clearly.
- Keep every document showing dates of default, payments and correspondence.
- Be careful about writing letters admitting the debt without advice.
- Do not rely on a writ petition to cure delay, because the Supreme Court in *United Bank of India v. Satyawati Tondon* (2010) 8 SCC 110 discouraged writs where the statutory remedy is available.

The Supreme Court in *Transcore v. Union of India* (2008) 1 SCC 125 held that SARFAESI and the RDB Act work together, so limitation questions may also arise in connected recovery proceedings.

## Getting advice

Limitation is fact-sensitive. A careful review of dates and documents is needed before deciding whether to raise it.
