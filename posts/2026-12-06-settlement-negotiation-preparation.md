One time settlement negotiation with a bank goes better when you prepare before the first conversation. A borrower who knows the exact dues, the status of the SARFAESI action and a realistic payment plan is in a stronger position to discuss terms. This article sets out the preparation that is useful, in plain steps.

## Why does preparation matter before negotiating?

A bank deciding on a settlement looks at the amount due, the value of the secured asset and the likelihood of recovery, under its own policy. It is not bound to accept any offer. What you can control is how clearly you understand your own position and how well you present a proposal.

Our post on [one-time settlement during SARFAESI proceedings](/blog/2026-10-09-one-time-settlement-sarfaesi) explains the general approach. This article focuses on what to do before you make an offer.

## What should you gather first?

Start by collecting the papers that show where the account stands:

- The loan sanction letter, loan agreement and security documents.
- Account statements from the date of the loan to date.
- The Section 13(2) notice, any possession notice and any sale notice you have received.
- Records of every payment you have made, with proof.

Ask the bank for a statement of dues in writing. Compare it with your own records and note any difference in interest, charges or credits, so that the figure under discussion is the correct one.

## How do you know where the SARFAESI action stands?

The stage of the action affects what is practical. Note the date of each notice and whether a Section 17 application is pending. If you have filed one, read about [settlement while a Section 17 application is pending](/blog/2026-11-19-section-17-settlement-while-pending), because the case must be closed properly once terms are agreed.

If an auction has been announced, the right of redemption under Section 13(8) is tied to the stage of the sale. Check the current text of the Act and the dates on the sale notice so that you do not lose that option while talking.

## How do you decide what you can offer?

Work out a figure you can actually pay, and how you will raise it. A proposal that you cannot fund causes more harm than none.

1. List the sources of funds, such as savings, sale of another asset or support from family, and the time each will take.
2. Decide whether you can pay in one instalment or need a schedule.
3. Keep a margin, so that a missed date does not undo the settlement.

Banks apply their own board-approved policies and the guidance of the Reserve Bank of India, which change over time. Check the current norms rather than assuming a standard formula.

## How should the proposal and talks be recorded?

Make the proposal in writing and keep a copy with proof of delivery. Record every meeting or call in a short note. Do not treat a verbal assurance as a settlement. Until the bank issues a written sanction of the terms, the enforcement steps may continue, so keep your replies and any challenge on track alongside the talks.

For the legal framework around payment and closure, see our page on [one-time settlement and redemption](/one-time-settlement-redemption).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I negotiate a one time settlement after receiving a SARFAESI notice?
Yes, a borrower may approach the bank at any stage. The bank decides under its own policy, and the notice period or later steps continue unless the bank agrees otherwise in writing.

### Should I stop replying to notices while negotiating?
No. Talks do not pause the legal timelines by themselves, so keep your rights protected and your replies on time.

### Is the bank bound to accept my offer?
No. A settlement exists only when both sides agree on terms, and the bank's sanction in writing is what you should rely on.
