When a bank takes possession of a mortgaged property under the SARFAESI Act 2002, the property may not be empty. It may be let out to a tenant, or used by a family member or a business. A common question is: **what happens to tenants and occupants?**

## Why the question arises

A mortgage is usually created by the owner, but a tenant may have a lease that was granted before or after the mortgage. The bank's right to possession comes from its security interest, while the tenant's right comes from the lease. The two can conflict.

## What the Supreme Court has said

In *Harshad Govardhan Sondagar v. International Asset Reconstruction Co.* (2014) 6 SCC 1, the Supreme Court dealt with the rights of lessees in the context of SARFAESI possession. The decision shows that a lessee's rights are examined on the facts and the law, and are not simply wiped out because a bank has taken action. Because the outcome depends on the lease and the circumstances, the full judgment and the **current text of the Act** should be read for the details.

## Documents that matter to a tenant

1. **The lease or rent agreement:** its date, term, rent and whether it was registered.
2. **Proof of payment:** rent receipts, bank statements and any deposit paid.
3. **Date of the mortgage:** whether the lease came before or after the bank's charge.
4. **Bank consent:** whether the lender knew of or agreed to the tenancy.
5. **Notices received:** any possession notice or Section 14 order served on the occupant.

## Practical steps for a tenant or occupant

- Do not ignore a notice that mentions your premises. Read it and note the date.
- Write to the bank, stating your status and attaching the lease and rent proofs.
- Do not pay rent to anyone else without understanding who is entitled to receive it.
- If the bank seeks help from a Magistrate under Section 14, find out whether you will be heard and whether you can place your documents on record.
- Where your rights are affected, you may be able to approach the Debts Recovery Tribunal, within the time limit in the Act.

## Practical steps for owners

An owner who has let out mortgaged property should keep the bank informed as the loan documents may require and should not treat a tenancy as a defence to a SARFAESI notice. The tenant's position and the borrower's position are different.

## Getting advice

Tenant rights depend on dates, documents and the exact facts of each case. Getting the lease and the bank's notices reviewed together is the best way to understand your position.
