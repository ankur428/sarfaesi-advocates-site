When a bank auction failed to produce a buyer, the secured creditor can usually try again with a fresh sale. A failed auction does not end the bank's security or the borrower's liability, and the property is generally put up for re-auction on a new notice. This article explains what a failed sale means and what borrowers and bidders should do next.

## Why does a bank auction fail?

An auction may fail for several practical reasons:

- No bidder came forward, or no bid reached the reserve price.
- The highest bidder did not pay the balance within the time allowed.
- The sale was stayed or cancelled before it was concluded.
- A defect in the sale notice meant the sale could not safely go ahead.

Each cause leads to a slightly different result, so look at what the bank actually records.

## What happens after a failed sale?

The secured creditor may sell the property again. A re-auction is a new sale, so the Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules 2002 apply to it afresh, including notice to the borrower and the publication of the sale notice. Check the current text of Rules 8 and 9 for the notice period and the steps. The earlier stages are laid out in our [SARFAESI auction timeline](/blog/2026-11-28-sarfaesi-auction-timeline).

The bank may also choose another mode of sale, such as tender or private treaty, where the Rules allow it. Our post on [methods of sale under Rule 8](/blog/2026-11-29-sale-of-secured-asset-methods-rule-8) explains those options.

## Can the reserve price be changed for the re-auction?

Banks often revise the reserve price when earlier attempts draw no bids. The reserve price is set by the secured creditor on the basis of a valuation, and any revision should be recorded and shown in the new notice. A borrower who believes the price is too low can raise it by objecting in writing and, where needed, in a proceeding under Section 17. See our note on [reserve price disputes](/blog/2026-10-11-reserve-price-disputes-sarfaesi-evidence).

## What should a borrower do?

1. Ask the authorised officer in writing why the sale did not conclude, and keep the reply.
2. Check that the new notice is served on you and states the date, reserve price and terms.
3. Compare the dues claimed with your own records, since interest keeps running.
4. Consider redemption or a settlement, which may still be possible before the sale is completed. Check the current text of Section 13(8) for the point up to which dues can be tendered.

A failed auction is often a useful window to approach the bank, but nothing in the Act obliges the bank to accept any particular offer.

## What should a bidder do?

A bidder whose sale failed should ask for the earnest money to be returned if the sale was cancelled or called off. Our article on [auction EMD refunds](/blog/2026-11-30-auction-earnest-money-refund) covers when a deposit is returned and when it can be forfeited. A bidder who intends to bid again should check the title and encumbrances afresh, as set out in the [buying at a SARFAESI auction checklist](/blog/2026-10-06-buying-at-sarfaesi-auction-checklist).

## Can a failed or repeated auction be challenged?

Yes, where the sale or the new notice does not follow the Rules. In Mathew Varghese v. M. Amritha Kumar (2014) 5 SCC 610, the Supreme Court stressed that Rules 8 and 9 are to be complied with. Our page on [SARFAESI auction disputes](/sarfaesi-auction-disputes) sets out the common grounds. The time limit for a Section 17 application is fixed by the Act, so check its current text.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the bank have to hold a fresh notice for a re-auction?
A re-auction is a new sale, so the Rules apply again, including notice to the borrower and publication of the sale notice. Check the current text of Rule 8 for the details.

### Does a failed auction reduce my loan?
No. The dues stay as they are, and interest continues as per the loan terms until the amount is paid or the property is sold. Ask the bank for an updated statement.

### Can the bank sell the property to one person without an auction after it fails?
Where the Rules allow another mode of sale, such as private treaty, the bank may use it, subject to the conditions in the Rules. Check the current text of Rule 8 for what is permitted.
