When a borrower does not hand over the secured asset voluntarily, the secured creditor can apply under Section 14 to the **Chief Metropolitan Magistrate** (in metropolitan areas) or the **District Magistrate** to take possession with official assistance.

## What the creditor must show

The creditor files an affidavit stating, among other things, that the account is an NPA, that a notice under Section 13(2) was issued, that the borrower has not objected or that the objection was rejected, and that the asset is within the territorial jurisdiction of the magistrate.

## Timeline

The Act, as amended in 2016, requires the magistrate to pass an order within **thirty days** of the application, which may be extended up to **sixty days** for recorded reasons.

## Role of the magistrate

The magistrate's role is largely administrative. The Supreme Court has explained that the magistrate is to verify that the affidavit's requirements are met, not to adjudicate the dispute on the merits. The merits are for the DRT under Section 17.

## Protection available to the borrower

- A Section 17 application can still be filed after possession is taken.
- Where the magistrate has not followed the Act, or possession is being taken of an asset outside the Act, a challenge can be considered.
- Residential occupants, tenants and family members should not be dispossessed without following the procedure. Lease and tenancy rights are considered separately, see *Harshad Govardhan Sondagar v. International Assets Reconstruction Co. Ltd.* (2014) 6 SCC 1.

Since interim orders from the DRT can be limited once possession is taken, timing is critical.
