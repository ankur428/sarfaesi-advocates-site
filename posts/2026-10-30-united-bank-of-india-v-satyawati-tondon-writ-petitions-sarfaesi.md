Borrowers facing SARFAESI action often ask whether they can go straight to the High Court under Article 226 of the Constitution. The Supreme Court addressed this in *United Bank of India v. Satyawati Tondon* (2010) 8 SCC 110, and the decision still shapes how High Courts treat such petitions.

## What the case decided

The Court discouraged High Courts from entertaining writ petitions against action taken under the SARFAESI Act when the Act itself gives the borrower a remedy before the Debts Recovery Tribunal (DRT). The central idea is that a writ court should not ordinarily bypass a statutory remedy that is available and effective.

## Why the Court took this view

The SARFAESI Act sets up a complete route for a borrower who is aggrieved by a measure taken by the bank: an application to the DRT under Section 17, and an appeal after that. Allowing parallel writ petitions would weaken that scheme and slow down recovery proceedings. The Court therefore treated the statutory remedy as the normal route.

## What this means in practice

- **Start with Section 17.** If the bank has taken a measure under Section 13(4), the usual remedy is an application before the DRT. Check the current text of the Act for the time limit and do not let it lapse.
- **A writ is not a shortcut.** Filing in the High Court first can cost time, and the court may send you back to the DRT.
- **Exceptions are narrow.** The High Court's power under Article 226 is not taken away, but it is used sparingly in this area. Whether your situation qualifies depends on its facts, so take advice before choosing this route.
- **Build the record early.** Whichever forum you use, keep your loan statements, notices, replies and proof of delivery in order.

## How it fits with other decisions

*Satyawati Tondon* should be read together with the other core judgments:

- *Mardia Chemicals v. Union of India* (2004) 4 SCC 311 upheld the Act's validity and recognised that a borrower can object to a notice, with the creditor required to reply with reasons.
- *Transcore v. Union of India* (2008) 1 SCC 125 explained that SARFAESI and the RDB Act operate together.
- *Mathew Varghese v. M. Amritha Kumar* (2014) 5 SCC 610 stressed that Rules 8 and 9 of the Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules must be complied with.

Together they point the same way: raise objections with the bank, use the DRT for challenges to enforcement, and hold the bank to the procedure the Act and Rules lay down.

## In short

Courts have been clear that SARFAESI disputes belong, in the ordinary course, before the DRT. If you have received a notice or the bank has taken a measure against your property, identify the statutory remedy and its deadline first.
