A SARFAESI notice reply is your written answer to the demand notice a bank sends under Section 13(2) of the SARFAESI Act 2002. A good reply is specific, polite and complete: it identifies what you dispute, asks for the records you need, and says what you are prepared to do. It also creates a record that you can rely on later if the matter reaches the Debts Recovery Tribunal.

## Why the reply matters

The Supreme Court in *Mardia Chemicals v. UOI* (2004) 4 SCC 311 recognised that a borrower can object to the notice and that the creditor must reply with reasons. A reply is how you use that right. Without one, the bank has nothing from you on record, and any objection you raise for the first time later may look like an afterthought.

The reply does not stop the clock by itself. The notice gives sixty days to discharge the liability, so plan to send the reply well within that period.

## What a good reply should include

Keep the structure simple so that the bank, and later a tribunal, can follow it without effort:

- your loan account number, the date of the notice and the amount it claims
- confirmation of the date and manner in which you received it
- each objection, numbered, with the entry, date or figure it relates to
- a request for statements of account, interest calculations and any documents the bank relies on
- your proposal, if any, for repayment or settlement
- a request that no measure be taken without following the Act and the Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules 2002

## Point out specific objections

General complaints such as "the amount is wrong" are easy to dismiss. Specific ones are harder to ignore. Examples include a payment not credited, interest charged at a rate you did not agree to, charges that are not explained, or a mismatch between the notice and the loan documents.

## Ask for reasons, not just a response

Ask the bank to deal with each objection and to give reasons if it rejects any of them. The Act provides in Section 13(3A) for the creditor to communicate its reasons; check the current text of the Act for the time allowed. Keep any reply you receive, because it will form part of the record if you later file an application under Section 17. You can read more about [the Section 13(2) notice reply](/section-13-2-notice-reply) and how it fits into the wider process.

## Practical points on sending it

Send the reply by a method that gives proof of delivery, such as registered post or courier with a tracking record, and keep a complete copy with enclosures. Where co-borrowers or guarantors are named in the notice, each should consider whether to reply separately or jointly. Stay courteous: an angry or accusatory tone adds nothing and can distract from the points that matter.

Do not use the reply to admit figures you have not checked. If you want to propose settlement, make clear that the proposal is without prejudice to your objections.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it compulsory to reply to a SARFAESI notice?
The Act does not make a reply mandatory, but it is wise to send one. It puts your objections on record and gives the bank a chance to explain its claim.

### Can I reply after the sixty days are over?
You can still write to the bank, but the measures under Section 13(4) become available to it once the notice period ends. A reply sent within the period is far more useful.

### Should I send the reply myself or through a lawyer?
Either is possible. What matters is that the reply is accurate, specific and sent with proof of delivery.
