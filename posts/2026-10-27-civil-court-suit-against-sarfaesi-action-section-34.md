A common reaction to a bank's SARFAESI notice is to file a suit in the local civil court. In most cases this is not the right forum. The SARFAESI Act 2002 has its own remedy, and it limits the role of the ordinary civil court.

## What Section 34 says in plain terms

Section 34 of the Act provides that a civil court has no jurisdiction to entertain a suit or proceeding in respect of any matter which a Debts Recovery Tribunal (DRT) or the Appellate Tribunal is empowered to decide under the Act. It also bars a civil court from granting an injunction against action taken under the Act. Check the current text of the Act for the exact wording.

## Where the dispute goes instead

A person aggrieved by a measure taken by the secured creditor under Section 13(4) can approach the DRT by way of an application under Section 17. A time limit applies, so check the current text of the Act for the period. A further appeal lies to the Debts Recovery Appellate Tribunal (DRAT) under Section 18, subject to the pre-deposit condition in that section.

The remedy is available to "any person" aggrieved, which can include a guarantor, a mortgagor, a lessee or another occupant, and not only the borrower.

## Why this matters in practice

- A suit filed in the wrong forum can waste time while the limit for a Section 17 application runs out.
- A civil court is likely to decline to hear matters that fall within the DRT's powers.
- Courts have said that the statutory remedy must ordinarily be used first. In *United Bank of India v. Satyawati Tondon* (2010) 8 SCC 110, the Supreme Court discouraged writ petitions against SARFAESI action where an effective alternative remedy exists.

## What to do on receiving a notice or possession action

- Read the notice and note the date of each step taken by the bank.
- Collect the loan documents, statements of account and any correspondence.
- Reply to the Section 13(2) notice with your objections in writing, as the bank is expected to consider them and respond with reasons.
- If a Section 13(4) measure is taken, consider a Section 17 application promptly rather than a civil suit.

## A word of caution

Section 34 does not mean that every dispute with a bank is outside the civil court. Matters that do not arise under the Act, or that the DRT cannot decide, may be treated differently, and the limits have been the subject of court decisions. Which forum is correct depends on the facts and the relief sought. This article is general information, not advice on any particular dispute.
