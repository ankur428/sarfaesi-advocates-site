A Section 18 appeal to the Debts Recovery Appellate Tribunal (DRAT) is filed against an order of the Debts Recovery Tribunal (DRT) passed under Section 17 of the SARFAESI Act 2002. A well-prepared appeal starts with the right documents and grounds that point to specific errors in the DRT order, so this checklist covers both.

## Who can file a Section 18 appeal?

Any person aggrieved by an order of the DRT under Section 17 may appeal. In most cases that means the borrower, but a guarantor, mortgagor or other party to the Section 17 application may also be affected. The secured creditor can appeal too, and the pre-deposit rule applies to the borrower's appeal, as explained in our article on [the pre-deposit requirement](/blog/2026-10-03-section-18-appeal-pre-deposit).

The appeal must be filed within thirty days of receiving the DRT order. Check the current text of Section 18 and the DRAT procedure rules for the position on delay.

## Which documents should be gathered?

An appeal is decided largely on the record that was before the DRT. Gather the following before drafting:

- A certified copy of the DRT order being challenged, with proof of the date it was received.
- The full Section 17 application with all annexures, and the secured creditor's reply.
- The Section 13(2) notice, the borrower's reply and the bank's response to it.
- Any possession notice, sale notice, valuation report and sale or auction papers.
- The loan agreement, security documents and account statements relied on before the DRT.
- Interim orders passed during the Section 17 proceedings.
- Proof of the pre-deposit, or the papers supporting an application to reduce it.
- A vakalatnama and the appeal memorandum in the form the DRAT rules require.

If a document was not placed before the DRT, ask whether it can properly be brought on record at the appeal stage. Check the current procedure rules before relying on new material.

## What grounds can be raised in the appeal?

The grounds should attack the reasoning of the DRT order, not simply repeat the Section 17 application. Common categories include:

1. The DRT overlooked a defect in the Section 13(2) notice or in the later steps. The Supreme Court in *Mardia Chemicals v. UOI* (2004) 4 SCC 311 recognised that the borrower can object and the creditor must reply with reasons.
2. The DRT did not consider whether Rules 8 and 9 of the Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules 2002 were complied with, as required by *Mathew Varghese v. M. Amritha Kumar* (2014) 5 SCC 610.
3. The DRT ignored material evidence or an argument that was properly raised before it.
4. The DRT dismissed the application as time-barred, or on another preliminary ground, without a proper basis on the record.
5. The order is not supported by reasons.

The appeal is not a fresh hearing of everything, so each ground should be tied to a page of the record. Our article on [grounds to challenge SARFAESI action](/blog/2026-11-17-section-17-grounds-to-challenge) explains the grounds that arise at the DRT stage.

## How should the pre-deposit be planned?

For a borrower's appeal, Section 18 makes a deposit a condition of the appeal being entertained. The DRAT may reduce it for reasons recorded in writing, within the limit the Act sets. Plan the funds and the supporting statements early, because the deposit is due with the appeal. For the full process, see our page on [Section 18 appeals before the DRAT](/section-18-drat-appeal).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I file a Section 18 appeal without the DRT order copy?
The order being challenged is the core of the appeal, so a copy is needed. Apply for a certified copy as soon as the order is pronounced, and keep proof of when it was received.

### Can the DRAT hear new grounds not argued before the DRT?
That depends on the facts and the DRAT's view of the record. It is safer to base the appeal on points that were raised before the DRT, and to check the current procedure rules for anything new.

### Does filing an appeal stop the bank from proceeding?
Not automatically. Any stay is a matter for the DRAT's order, so check the terms of the interim orders in force.
