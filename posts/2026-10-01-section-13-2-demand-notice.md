A notice under Section 13(2) is the first formal step in SARFAESI enforcement. It calls on the borrower to discharge the liability **in full within sixty days**, failing which the secured creditor may proceed under Section 13(4).

## What the notice must contain

Under Section 13(3), the notice must give the details of the amount payable and the secured assets intended to be enforced. Check the amount, the date of NPA classification, the security described and whether the notice went to every borrower and guarantor.

## Your first steps

1. **Note the date of receipt.** The sixty days are counted from it.
2. **Collect the loan documents:** sanction letter, loan agreement, security documents, statements of account and earlier correspondence.
3. **Reconcile the account.** Interest, penal charges and fees frequently need checking.
4. **Send a written reply or objection.** Under Section 13(3A), if the borrower makes a representation or objection, the secured creditor must communicate its reasons for not accepting it within fifteen days.
5. **Do not transfer or deal with the secured asset.** Section 13(13) bars the borrower from doing so after receipt of the notice, without the creditor's consent.

## Why the reply matters

The reasons given under Section 13(3A) can be challenged later. A properly framed reply creates a record that can support an application before the Tribunal. Note that the Supreme Court has held in *Mardia Chemicals* that the borrower may raise objections at this stage and that the creditor must respond with reasons.

## Settlement window

This is also the most practical time to negotiate a restructuring or one-time settlement, before possession and auction costs are added to the dues.
