A borrower who wants to set aside a SARFAESI sale must approach the Debts Recovery Tribunal (DRT) by an application under Section 17 of the SARFAESI Act 2002. The Tribunal can examine whether the sale followed the Act and the Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules 2002, and if it did not, it has power to declare the steps invalid and pass consequential orders.

## Which forum hears a challenge to a bank sale?

A challenge to measures taken by a secured creditor under Section 13(4) goes to the DRT. Civil courts are barred from entertaining such matters, as explained in our article on [the civil court bar under Section 34](/blog/2026-10-27-civil-court-suit-against-sarfaesi-action-section-34). A writ petition is possible only in limited cases, since *United Bank of India v. Satyawati Tondon* (2010) 8 SCC 110 discourages writs where the Section 17 remedy exists.

For the filing process itself, see our guide to [Section 17 applications](/section-17-drt-application).

## On what grounds can a sale be challenged?

The Tribunal looks at whether the secured creditor complied with the law before and during the sale. Common grounds include:

- The Section 13(2) notice or the Section 13(4) steps were not valid.
- The sale notice was not given to the borrower as the Rules require.
- The mandatory notice period before the sale was not observed.
- The property was not properly described or valued, or the reserve price was set without a sound basis.
- The sale was not conducted by one of the methods allowed by Rule 8.

The Supreme Court held in *Mathew Varghese v. M. Amritha Kumar* (2014) 5 SCC 610 that Rules 8 and 9 must be complied with. Our article on [Rules 8 and 9 compliance](/blog/2026-10-24-mathew-varghese-rules-8-9-compliance) explains this in more detail.

## What orders can the DRT pass?

Under Section 17(3), if the Tribunal finds that the measures taken were not in accordance with the Act and the Rules, it can declare them invalid. It may then restore possession or management to the borrower, or pass other orders as it considers appropriate. Under Section 17(4), where the measures are found to be in accordance with the law, the Tribunal may allow the secured creditor to proceed.

In practice, the orders a borrower may ask for are:

1. A declaration that the sale or the steps leading to it are invalid.
2. Restoration of possession of the secured asset, where it has been taken.
3. Directions on the money paid by the bidder, or other consequential relief.

What the Tribunal will actually order depends on the facts of each case, including how far the sale has gone and who now holds rights in the property. No result can be assumed.

## Does it matter whether the sale is complete?

It can. A challenge made before the sale is confirmed is generally easier to frame than one made after a sale certificate has been issued and a third party has paid the price. A purchaser who has acted in good faith has rights too, and our piece on [the purchaser's rights under Section 13(6)](/blog/2026-11-01-section-13-6-rights-of-purchaser-after-bank-sale) explains why.

Delay also counts. Section 17 sets a limit for filing, so check the current text of the Act for the period and when it starts.

## Does the borrower have to pay anything to be heard?

Interim relief and the conditions attached to it are decided by the Tribunal. Our article on [interim relief under Section 17](/blog/2026-10-14-interim-relief-section-17-application-deposit) covers what to expect. Check the current Act and the Tribunal's directions for any deposit requirement.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I challenge a bank auction after it has taken place?
Yes, an application under Section 17 can be filed after the sale, but it must be within the time allowed. The Tribunal will look at whether the Act and Rules were followed.

### Will the DRT always cancel a sale that has a defect?
Not necessarily. The Tribunal considers the nature of the defect, the stage of the sale and the interests of those involved. Some defects are serious, while others may not change the result.

### Who is a party in an application to set aside a sale?
The bank or secured creditor is a necessary party. The auction purchaser is also commonly joined, because any order may affect the purchaser's rights.
