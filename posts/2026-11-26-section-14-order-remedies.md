If you have received a Section 14 order, the magistrate has directed that possession of the secured asset be taken and handed to the bank. A Section 14 order what to do question is best answered in order: read the order, gather your papers, and look at the remedies the Act provides, mainly an application to the Debts Recovery Tribunal (DRT) under Section 17.

## What does a Section 14 order mean?

The order is made by the Chief Metropolitan Magistrate or the District Magistrate on the bank's application. It directs that possession be taken and forwarded to the secured creditor, usually with the help of a court-appointed officer or the police. Our article on [how courts assist banks under Section 14](/blog/2026-10-04-section-14-possession) explains the stage in more detail.

The order is not a ruling that the debt is correct. The magistrate's role is limited, as discussed in our note on [what a bank must file under Section 14](/blog/2026-11-25-section-14-application-contents).

## First steps after receiving the order

Do not ignore the order or the date fixed for possession. A calm and documented response is more useful than a hurried one. Consider the following:

1. Read the order and note the date, the property described and the officer named to carry it out.
2. Collect the Section 13(2) notice, the possession notice, any reply you sent and the bank's response.
3. Collect proof of who lives in or uses the property, such as a lease, rent receipts or utility bills.
4. Make a list of movable goods and documents inside the property.
5. Seek legal advice quickly, because the period for a Section 17 application runs from the date of the measure.

## What remedies are available?

The main remedy is an application under Section 17 to the DRT. Any person aggrieved by measures taken by the secured creditor can apply, and this includes a borrower, a guarantor and, in suitable cases, an occupant. The Tribunal can examine whether the bank followed the Act and the Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules 2002. Our page on [Section 17 applications](/section-17-drt-application) sets out how this works. The time limit for filing is fixed by the Act, so check the current text of Section 17.

The case law is clear that a measure under the Act should ordinarily be taken to the Tribunal first. In United Bank of India v. Satyawati Tondon (2010) 8 SCC 110, the Supreme Court discouraged writ petitions against action under the Act where an efficacious remedy exists. A High Court may still be approached in rare situations, but that is the exception.

A borrower who wishes to settle can also approach the bank. Redemption of the secured asset and one-time settlement are separate options and do not replace the need to protect legal remedies in time.

## What should occupants and tenants remember?

An occupant who is not the borrower may have rights that differ from the borrower's. A lessee, for example, may have a protected interest, as discussed in Harshad Govardhan Sondagar v. IARC (2014) 6 SCC 1. Occupants should keep their lease papers safe and seek advice before vacating.

Where possession is to be taken, ask for a list of the items removed and keep a copy. If household goods or documents are involved, note them in writing.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I challenge a Section 14 order directly?
The usual route is a Section 17 application to the DRT against the measures taken by the bank. Writ petitions are discouraged where that remedy exists, so a direct challenge should be considered with legal advice.

### Does a Section 14 order mean the property is sold immediately?
No. The order relates to possession. A sale follows its own steps under the Rules, and the borrower can raise defects before the Tribunal. Our article on [taking possession under Section 13(4)(a)](/blog/2026-11-08-section-13-4-a-take-possession-of-secured-asset) explains the difference between the two routes.

### What if I am a tenant and not the borrower?
Gather your lease and rent records at once. Your position depends on the lease and the facts, so take advice before vacating.
