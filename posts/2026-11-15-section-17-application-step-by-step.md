The Section 17 application procedure begins when a secured creditor takes a measure under Section 13(4) of the SARFAESI Act 2002 and ends with a decision by the Debts Recovery Tribunal (DRT). Knowing the order of steps helps you prepare papers early and avoid avoidable delay. This guide walks through them in plain terms.

## Step 1: Identify the measure being challenged

A Section 17 application is directed at a specific step taken by the bank, such as taking possession, taking over management, or a sale. Write down which measure was taken, on what date, and under which provision. Keep the possession notice, sale notice or any other communication that shows it.

Anyone whose rights are affected may apply, not only the borrower. Our note on [who can file a Section 17 application](/blog/2026-10-31-who-can-file-section-17-application-drt) explains this in more detail.

## Step 2: Check the time limit

The application must be made within the period the Act prescribes, counted from the date of the measure. Check the current text of the Act for the exact period and how it is counted. Our earlier article on the [Section 17 application and its time limit](/blog/2026-10-02-section-17-application-drt) covers the point.

Do not wait for settlement talks to conclude. Talks with the bank do not by themselves stop the period running.

## Step 3: Collect documents and plan your grounds

Gather the loan papers, the Section 13(2) notice, your reply and the bank's response, the possession or sale notices, and loan statements. Then list your grounds one by one, for example a defect in the notice, a wrong amount, or non-compliance with Rules 8 and 9 of the Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules 2002. *Mathew Varghese v. M. Amritha Kumar* (2014) 5 SCC 610 stresses that these Rules must be complied with.

## Step 4: Draft and file the application

The application is filed in the DRT that has jurisdiction over the matter. In general it contains:

1. The names and addresses of the parties.
2. The facts in date order.
3. The measure challenged and the grounds for challenging it.
4. The relief sought, including any interim relief.
5. Copies of the supporting documents, with a list.

Court fee and filing requirements are set by the rules in force, so check the current rules and the Tribunal's filing practice before you file.

## Step 5: Service, reply and hearings

After filing, the Tribunal issues notice to the bank. The bank files a reply, and the applicant may respond to it. You can ask for interim protection against possession or sale at an early hearing, and the Tribunal decides whether to grant it and on what terms.

Keep your account position ready, as the Tribunal may ask what you are able to deposit. The Tribunal then hears both sides on the documents and arguments.

## What happens after the decision

If the Tribunal finds the measure invalid, it can grant relief under the Act. If the application is dismissed, a further remedy by appeal may be available, so read the order carefully and check the time allowed for it. See our page on [Section 17 applications before the DRT](/section-17-drt-application) for the wider picture.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I file a Section 17 application without a lawyer?
The Act does not make it impossible to appear yourself, but the papers are technical. Many applicants seek legal advice so that the grounds and documents are set out correctly.

### Do I have to pay the bank before filing?
There is no general rule that payment must come first for filing a Section 17 application. Interim protection, however, is discretionary and the Tribunal may attach conditions, so check the current position for your case.

### Can the application be filed after the property is sold?
A sale is itself a measure that may be challenged, but the time limit and the rights of a purchaser matter. Seek advice on your papers without delay.
