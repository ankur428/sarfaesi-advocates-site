The reserve price is the minimum price below which the bank will not sell the property in a SARFAESI auction. Borrowers often feel that the reserve price is too low and ask whether it can be challenged. Such a challenge usually depends on evidence.

## How the reserve price is set

Under the Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules 2002, the authorised officer of the bank obtains a valuation of the property and fixes a reserve price before the sale. The auction notice must state the details the Rules require. **Check the current text of Rules 8 and 9** for the exact steps. The Supreme Court in *Mathew Varghese v. M. Amritha Kumar* (2014) 5 SCC 610 said that Rules 8 and 9 must be complied with, which gives a borrower a basis to examine the process.

## Grounds a borrower may examine

1. Was the **valuation** done by a qualified valuer, and is the report available?
2. Does the report describe the **correct property**, area and location?
3. Was the reserve price fixed after the valuation, and is the working shown?
4. Was the **sale notice** issued with the required details and gap in time?
5. Was the borrower **informed** as the Rules require?

## Evidence that can help

- **An independent valuation report** from a registered valuer, prepared close to the date of the auction.
- **Registered sale deeds** of comparable properties nearby.
- **Government circle rates or guideline values**, where relevant.
- **Photographs and measurements** of the property.
- **Records of earlier valuations**, including the valuation at the time of the loan.
- **Copies of all notices**, bank letters and emails.

A claim that the price is "too low" without such material is difficult to establish.

## Where to raise it

A challenge to a sale conducted under the Act is raised by way of a **Section 17 application** before the Debts Recovery Tribunal, within the limit in the Act. The Supreme Court in *United Bank of India v. Satyawati Tondon* (2010) 8 SCC 110 discouraged writ petitions where this remedy is available.

## Practical tips

- Act before the auction date, if possible, instead of after the sale.
- Ask the bank in writing for the valuation report and the basis of the reserve price.
- If a better offer is available, put it in writing and share proof of the buyer's capacity.
- Do not rely on informal assurances.

The post on bank auction notices on this blog explains the notice requirements in more detail.

## Getting advice

Reserve price disputes turn on valuation evidence and the facts of the sale. An early document review helps in deciding whether a challenge is worth pursuing.
