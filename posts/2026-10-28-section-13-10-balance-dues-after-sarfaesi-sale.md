A SARFAESI sale does not always close the account. If the price realised at auction is lower than the amount due, a borrower may still face a claim for the shortfall. Section 13(10) of the SARFAESI Act 2002 deals with this situation.

## What Section 13(10) provides

In plain terms, where the dues of the secured creditor are not fully satisfied from the sale proceeds of the secured assets, the secured creditor may apply to the Debts Recovery Tribunal (DRT) having jurisdiction, or a competent court, for recovery of the balance amount from the borrower. Check the current text of the Act and the Rules for the exact wording and the prescribed form and manner of the application.

## How this connects to the RDB Act

The balance is not recovered by a further SARFAESI sale of the same asset. It is pursued through the recovery machinery of the Recovery of Debts and Bankruptcy Act 1993 (RDB Act). The Supreme Court in *Transcore v. Union of India* (2008) 1 SCC 125 explained that the SARFAESI Act and the RDB Act operate together and that a creditor's remedies under them are meant to work in a coordinated way.

## What a borrower or guarantor should check

- **The statement of account.** Ask for a clear break-up of the amount due, the sale proceeds, the costs charged and the balance claimed.
- **Compliance with the Rules.** If notice, possession or sale steps were not properly followed, that may bear on the sale itself. In *Mathew Varghese v. M. Amritha Kumar* (2014) 5 SCC 610, the Supreme Court stressed that Rules 8 and 9 must be complied with.
- **Valuation and price.** A sale at a very low price can increase the shortfall, so the valuation and reserve price are worth examining.
- **Your own capacity.** A guarantor's liability for the balance depends on the guarantee documents, so these should be read carefully.

## Challenging the sale or the claim

A person aggrieved by a measure taken under Section 13(4) may approach the DRT under Section 17, subject to the time limit in the Act. The claim for the balance is a separate proceeding, in which the borrower can put forward defences on the amount and the accounts. Because timelines apply, it is sensible to act early.

## In short

A SARFAESI auction may end the bank's hold on the property but not necessarily its claim for money. If you receive a notice or application for a shortfall, read it closely, gather the loan and sale records, and take advice on the facts of your case. This article is general information, not advice on any particular dispute.
