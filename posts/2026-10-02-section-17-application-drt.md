Section 17 gives any person, including a borrower or guarantor, whose rights are affected by a measure under Section 13(4) the right to apply to the Debts Recovery Tribunal (DRT).

## Limitation

The application must be filed **within forty-five days** from the date on which the measure was taken. The DRT is expected to decide the application within sixty days, extendable up to four months in total, though in practice matters run longer.

## Grounds commonly raised

- The account was wrongly classified as an NPA, or the notice was defective.
- The demand notice was not served properly or the amount was wrongly computed.
- The secured creditor did not answer the objections under Section 13(3A) with reasons.
- The possession or sale notices did not comply with the Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules, 2002.
- The property was undervalued or the reserve price was unreasonable.
- The asset falls outside the Act, for example agricultural land.

## What the Tribunal can do

If the DRT finds that the measures did not follow the Act, it can declare them invalid and restore possession or management to the borrower under Section 17(3), or grant other appropriate relief. If it finds them valid, the creditor may proceed.

## Interim protection

Interim relief against possession or sale is discretionary. Tribunals often ask for a part-payment or deposit as a condition. Prepare the financial position before the first hearing.

## Practical points

File as early as possible, attach the notices and proof of receipt, and separate the jurisdictional grounds from disputes on the amount owed. Counter-claims and set-offs are permitted in Section 17 proceedings, which is why a careful account reconciliation matters.
