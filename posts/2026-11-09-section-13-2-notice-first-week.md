If you have just received a demand notice from your bank, the question of SARFAESI notice what to do first has a practical answer: stay calm, record the date of receipt, gather your loan papers and start preparing a written reply. The notice under Section 13(2) of the SARFAESI Act 2002 gives you sixty days to pay, and the first week is best used to get organised rather than to react.

## Day one: record how and when the notice arrived

Write down the date and manner of receipt, and keep the envelope, courier slip or email. The sixty-day period runs from receipt of the notice, so this date matters later if any dispute arises about timing.

Read the notice slowly. Note the amount claimed, the date the account was classified as a non-performing asset, and the assets the bank says it will enforce. Also check that your name, and the names of any co-borrowers and guarantors, are stated correctly.

## Days two and three: collect your papers

Bring together everything connected with the loan. A basic set includes:

- the sanction letter and loan agreement
- the mortgage or security documents and any guarantee deed
- statements of account from the date of disbursal
- receipts and proof of every payment made
- earlier letters, emails and messages exchanged with the bank

If a document is missing, ask the bank for a copy in writing and keep a note of the request. Our guide to [documents to keep ready](/blog/2026-11-04-documents-to-keep-ready-sarfaesi-notice) covers this in more detail.

## Days four and five: check the figures

Compare the amount claimed with your own records. Look at how interest has been charged, whether penal charges and fees are explained, and whether every payment you made has been credited. A step-by-step approach is set out in [checking your loan account before replying](/blog/2026-10-20-checking-loan-account-before-replying-sarfaesi-notice).

Make a short list of every point you dispute, with the date, entry or figure it relates to. Specific points are far more useful than general complaints.

## Days six and seven: decide how to respond

Once the papers and figures are in order, decide on your approach. In *Mardia Chemicals v. UOI* (2004) 4 SCC 311, the Supreme Court recognised that a borrower can object to the notice and that the creditor must reply with reasons. The Act now provides for this in Section 13(3A); check the current text for the time allowed to the bank.

Your written reply can set out your objections, ask for supporting records, and, if you wish, propose repayment or settlement. Send it so that you have proof of delivery. More on what the reply should cover is in our page on [replying to a Section 13(2) notice](/section-13-2-notice-reply).

## What to avoid during this period

Do not sell, lease or otherwise transfer the secured asset after receiving the notice. Section 13(13) restricts this without the secured creditor's prior written consent. Do not ignore the notice in the hope that it will go away, because the bank may take measures under Section 13(4) once the sixty days end.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have to reply to a SARFAESI notice within the first week?
No. The notice gives sixty days to discharge the liability. The first week is simply a sensible time to organise papers so that your reply is accurate and complete.

### Can I keep living in the property after getting the notice?
The notice itself does not take possession of the property. Possession is a later step that the bank can take only through the procedure laid down in the Act and Rules.

### What if the bank's figures look wrong?
Say so in your written reply, pointing to the specific entries you dispute and attaching your records. The bank is expected to respond to your objections with reasons.
