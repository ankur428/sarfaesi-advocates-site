When people think of SARFAESI enforcement, they usually picture possession and auction. Section 13(4) lists several separate measures, and one of them is the appointment of a manager. It is easy to overlook, yet it can affect how a property or business asset is run while the dispute continues. As the provisions have been amended over time, always check the current text of the Act.

## What the measure is

If the borrower does not discharge the liability within the notice period under Section 13(2), the secured creditor may choose from the measures listed in Section 13(4). Clause (c) allows the creditor to appoint any person as manager of the secured assets whose possession it has taken. In other words, it follows possession. A manager cannot be appointed under this clause for an asset the bank has not yet taken over.

## How it differs from other Section 13(4) measures

- **Clause (a)** deals with taking possession of the secured assets, including the right to transfer them by lease, assignment or sale.
- **Clause (b)** deals with taking over the management of the borrower's business.
- **Clause (c)** deals with appointing a manager for the secured assets already taken into possession.
- **Clause (d)** deals with requiring certain persons who owe money to the borrower to pay the creditor.

## Why it matters in practice

A secured asset such as a let-out building, a factory or a commercial unit may need day-to-day care between possession and sale. A manager may look after the asset so that its value is preserved for the benefit of the recovery process. The bank remains bound by the Act and the Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules, 2002. The Supreme Court in *Mathew Varghese v. M. Amritha Kumar* (2014) 5 SCC 610 stressed that Rules 8 and 9 must be complied with in enforcement.

## What a borrower or occupant can check

- Whether a valid notice under Section 13(2) was issued and the borrower's reply was considered.
- Whether the bank actually took possession before appointing the manager.
- Who the manager is and in what capacity the manager is acting.
- Whether the steps followed the Rules, and whether records and notices were properly served.

## Remedies

A borrower or any person aggrieved by a measure under Section 13(4) can consider a Section 17 application before the Debts Recovery Tribunal, within the time limit stated in the Act. In *Mardia Chemicals v. Union of India* (2004) 4 SCC 311, the Supreme Court recognised that a borrower can raise objections and the creditor must reply with reasons.

Writ petitions are not usually the first step. In *United Bank of India v. Satyawati Tondon* (2010) 8 SCC 110, the Supreme Court discouraged writs where an effective statutory remedy exists.

## In short

Section 13(4)(c) lets a secured creditor appoint a manager for secured assets it has already taken into possession. If you are affected, keep every notice and record, check the steps against the Act and the Rules, and seek advice early, since time limits are short.
