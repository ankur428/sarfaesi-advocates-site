The SARFAESI auction timeline runs in a fixed order: possession, valuation, notice of sale, the sale itself, payment of the price, confirmation and finally the sale certificate. The Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules 2002 set the steps and several minimum periods, and a bank that skips one risks having the sale challenged. This article walks through the sequence so that borrowers, occupants and bidders can see where a sale stands.

## What must happen before a sale can be planned?

A secured creditor cannot move to sale straight after the Section 13(2) notice. The notice period must first expire, and the creditor must then take a measure under Section 13(4), usually possession of the secured asset. Possession is taken through a notice under Rule 8, as described in our note on the [possession notice under Rule 8](/blog/2026-10-03-possession-notice-rule-8-symbolic-physical).

After possession, the authorised officer obtains a valuation of the asset and fixes a reserve price in consultation with the secured creditor. Our article on [the bank auction notice and reserve price](/blog/2026-10-05-bank-auction-notice-reserve-price) explains what the notice should disclose.

## The main steps in order

1. Section 13(2) demand notice and the expiry of the notice period.
2. Possession of the secured asset under Section 13(4), by notice under Rule 8.
3. Valuation and fixing of the reserve price.
4. Notice of sale to the borrower and publication of the sale notice.
5. The sale itself, whether by auction, tender or another permitted method.
6. Payment of the price in the stages and periods the Rules prescribe.
7. Confirmation of the sale and issue of the sale certificate.

Each stage depends on the one before it. A defect at an early stage, such as a notice that was never properly served, can affect everything that follows.

## How long must the notice of sale run?

The Rules require notice of sale to the borrower and publication of a public notice, and the sale cannot take place until thirty days have passed from that notice. The thirty days are meant to give the borrower time to pay the dues or to arrange a better bid. The exact wording, including how the period is counted, should be checked against the current text of Rule 8 and Rule 9.

The notice must give details such as the description of the asset, the reserve price, the time and place of the sale and the terms of payment. The Supreme Court stressed in Mathew Varghese v. M. Amritha Kumar (2014) 5 SCC 610 that Rules 8 and 9 are to be complied with. For that reason, a sale held before the full period has run, or on a notice that omits required details, is open to objection.

## What happens after the bid is accepted?

Once the highest bid is accepted, the Rules require the bidder to pay a portion of the price promptly and the balance within a fixed period, which may be extended by written agreement within the limit the Rules allow. Check the current text of Rule 9 for the percentages and days. If the bidder does not pay, the deposit can be forfeited and the asset can be put up for sale again.

When the full price is paid and the sale is confirmed, the authorised officer issues a sale certificate in the form the Rules provide. The certificate is the document the purchaser uses to complete registration and mutation. Our guide to [buying at a SARFAESI auction](/blog/2026-10-06-buying-at-sarfaesi-auction-checklist) lists what a bidder should verify before and after the sale.

## Where can a borrower challenge the sale?

A borrower or other aggrieved person can apply to the Debts Recovery Tribunal under Section 17. Many sale disputes turn on whether the timeline above was followed, which is why dates matter. Our page on [SARFAESI auction disputes](/sarfaesi-auction-disputes) sets out the common grounds. The period for filing is fixed by the Act, so check the current text of Section 17 and act promptly.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does a SARFAESI auction take from start to finish?
There is no single figure, because the time depends on how soon the bank takes possession, completes valuation and publishes the sale notice. The Rules fix certain minimum periods, but the total varies from case to case.

### Can the bank auction my property without telling me?
No. The Rules require notice of sale to the borrower, in addition to public notice. A sale held without the required notice can be challenged.

### When does the purchaser become the owner?
The purchaser's title is evidenced by the sale certificate, issued after the price is paid and the sale is confirmed. Registration and mutation then follow under the applicable law.
