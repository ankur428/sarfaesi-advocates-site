A one-time settlement (OTS) lets a borrower clear an NPA account by paying a negotiated amount. Banks follow their board-approved recovery policies and RBI directions on compromise settlements, and the terms vary between lenders.

## When to approach the bank

The stronger position is **early**: soon after the Section 13(2) notice, before possession and auction costs are added. Pursuing a Section 17 application alongside negotiation often improves the bank's willingness to discuss.

## Preparing the proposal

1. Reconcile the account and identify the principal, interest and charges.
2. Decide the amount and the source of funds you can realistically pay.
3. Send a written proposal with the payment schedule, and ask the bank for its acknowledgment.
4. Ask for the bank's written sanction, listing the amount, timeline and consequences of default.

## Points to include in the settlement

- A statement that the amount is in **full and final settlement** of the loan and all related claims.
- Withdrawal or closure of the enforcement steps and any pending proceedings, on payment.
- **Release of security** and return of original documents, with a no-dues certificate.
- Consequences of default, including whether the bank may revive the original claim.
- Treatment of guarantors, who should also be released.

## Pending proceedings

If a matter is pending before the DRT or DRAT, record the settlement through a compromise application or a joint memo, so that the order reflects it. Where a sale notice has been published, the right of redemption under Section 13(8) may already have ended, so the bank's sanction becomes essential.
