Not every secured loan or every asset can be enforced under the SARFAESI Act. Section 31 lists certain exemptions, and a borrower who receives a notice should check whether the asset or the loan falls within them. Always read the current text of Section 31, as the list is specific and has been amended over time.

## Why this question matters

The Act gives a secured creditor the power to enforce security without going to court. That power exists only where the Act applies. If the asset or the security interest is outside the Act, the proper route for the creditor may be different, such as a civil suit or an application before the Debts Recovery Tribunal under the RDB Act. The Supreme Court in *Transcore v. Union of India* (2008) 1 SCC 125 recognised that SARFAESI and the RDB Act operate together, so a creditor who cannot use one route may have the other.

## What Section 31 covers

In general terms, Section 31 provides that the enforcement provisions of Section 13 do not apply to certain categories. One well-known example is agricultural land. Other categories are also listed, including certain kinds of security interests and certain small-value cases. Because the details matter, check the current text of the Act rather than relying on a summary.

## Practical steps for a borrower

- Read the Section 13(2) notice and identify exactly which assets it describes as secured assets.
- Compare each asset against the categories in Section 31.
- If any asset appears to be covered by an exemption, say so in a written reply to the bank. The Supreme Court in *Mardia Chemicals v. Union of India* (2004) 4 SCC 311 recognised that a borrower can raise objections and that the creditor must respond with reasons.
- Keep a copy of the reply and proof of delivery.

## If the bank proceeds anyway

A person aggrieved by a measure taken under Section 13(4) may consider an application under Section 17 before the Debts Recovery Tribunal, within the time limit in the Act. The Tribunal can examine whether the measures taken were in accordance with the Act, which includes whether the Act applied to the asset at all.

Writ petitions are not usually the first choice. In *United Bank of India v. Satyawati Tondon* (2010) 8 SCC 110, the Supreme Court discouraged writs where an effective statutory remedy exists.

## A note on documents

An asset may be mortgaged along with others. A notice that lists several properties can include some that are exempt and some that are not. Each should be examined separately, with the title documents and the loan papers in hand.

## In short

Section 31 is a threshold question. Before debating the amount due or the auction, confirm that the Act covers the asset in question. A careful first read of the notice and the exemptions can shape every later step.
