The most common SARFAESI notice mistakes are ignoring the notice, missing the sixty-day period and replying without checking the figures. A demand under Section 13(2) of the SARFAESI Act 2002 starts a clock, and what you do in that period shapes your later options. Knowing the usual errors helps you avoid them.

## Mistake 1: Ignoring the notice or leaving it unopened

Some borrowers put the envelope aside, hoping the matter will settle itself. The notice asks you to discharge your liability in full within sixty days, failing which the secured creditor may take measures under Section 13(4). Silence does not stop that process.

Note the date you received it and read it fully. Our guide to the [first week after a SARFAESI notice](/blog/2026-11-09-section-13-2-notice-first-week) lists what to do straight away.

## Mistake 2: Losing track of the sixty-day period

The period is short and runs from receipt. Borrowers often count from the date printed on the letter, or assume a visit to the branch has paused the period. Keep proof of when and how the notice reached you, and put the last date in your calendar.

Check the current text of the Act for the period and for any later steps. Do not assume that talks with the bank extend the time unless the bank confirms it in writing.

## Mistake 3: Replying without checking the account

A reply that disputes everything, or admits everything, can hurt you. Before you write, compare the amount demanded with your loan statements, sanction letter and payment receipts. Interest, charges and the date of classification as a non-performing asset should all be traceable.

*Mardia Chemicals v. UOI* (2004) 4 SCC 311 recognised that a borrower can object to the notice and that the creditor must reply with reasons. That right is only useful if your objection is specific.

## Mistake 4: Relying on phone calls instead of a written reply

Verbal assurances from a branch officer are hard to prove later. A written reply, sent in a way that gives you a record of delivery, puts your objections on file. See [what a good reply should cover](/blog/2026-11-10-section-13-2-notice-reply-contents) for the points to include.

Our page on the [Section 13(2) notice reply](/section-13-2-notice-reply) explains how the reply fits into the wider process.

## Other slips worth avoiding

- Transferring or creating a charge on the secured asset after the notice, which Section 13(13) restricts without the creditor's consent.
- Signing a settlement or acknowledgment you have not read.
- Forgetting co-borrowers and guarantors, who may each need to reply.
- Waiting for the bank to act before seeking advice on the remedies available.

## Frequently asked questions

### What happens if I make one of these SARFAESI notice mistakes?
It depends on the stage and the facts. Some errors can be corrected by a prompt written reply, while others, such as missing a later deadline, may narrow your options, so seek advice on your own papers.

### Is it a mistake to pay part of the demand?
Not necessarily, but a part payment does not by itself end the notice. Ask the bank in writing how it will treat the payment and whether it changes the steps it proposes to take.

### Can I reply after the sixty days have passed?
You can still write to the bank, but the objection is most useful within the notice period. Check the current text of the Act for what follows once that period ends.
