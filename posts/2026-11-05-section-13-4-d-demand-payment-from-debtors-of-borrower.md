Most people link SARFAESI action with possession of property or an auction. Section 13(4) also lists a measure that reaches money owed to the borrower: the secured creditor may require, by notice, certain persons who owe money to the borrower to pay that money to the bank instead. Because the details have been amended over time, always check the current text of the Act.

## What the measure is

Under Section 13(4), if the borrower does not discharge the liability within the notice period under Section 13(2), the secured creditor may choose among the listed measures. One of them allows the creditor to require, by written notice, a person who has acquired any of the secured assets from the borrower, or from whom money is due or may become due to the borrower, to pay the creditor. The amount is to be set against the borrower's liability. The measure relates to the secured assets and to the amounts payable in respect of them.

## Where it is relevant

This measure is most often discussed where the secured asset generates payments. Examples include rent from a let-out property or amounts payable to the borrower by a buyer of the secured asset. It is only one of several options and is not used in every account.

## If you receive such a notice

A tenant, customer or other payer who receives a bank notice of this kind may wish to:

- Read the notice carefully and note the date it was received.
- Check who is sending it, and whether it refers to a loan and a secured asset that actually connect to the amounts you pay.
- Compare it with your own agreement with the borrower, such as a lease or sale agreement.
- Keep proof of every payment made, before and after the notice.
- Avoid making payments to anyone without understanding which notice applies.

## Rights of the borrower

A borrower who believes the measure is wrongly invoked can consider a Section 17 application before the Debts Recovery Tribunal, within the time limit in the Act. The Tribunal can examine whether the measure was taken in accordance with the Act and the Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules, 2002.

The borrower may also point to the objection route recognised in *Mardia Chemicals v. Union of India* (2004) 4 SCC 311, where the Supreme Court held that a borrower can raise objections and the creditor must reply with reasons.

Writ petitions are not usually the first step. In *United Bank of India v. Satyawati Tondon* (2010) 8 SCC 110, the Supreme Court discouraged writs where an effective statutory remedy exists.

## In short

Section 13(4) lets a secured creditor look beyond the property itself to money payable to the borrower in respect of the secured assets. Both the borrower and the person asked to pay should read the notice closely, keep clear records and seek advice early, since time limits are short.
