An order of the Debts Recovery Tribunal under Section 17 can be challenged before the Debts Recovery Appellate Tribunal (DRAT) under Section 18.

## Time limit

The appeal must be filed within **thirty days** of receiving the DRT's order. The DRAT can condone delay in appropriate cases, but that should never be planned for.

## The pre-deposit

Where the **borrower** appeals, Section 18(1) requires a deposit of **fifty per cent of the debt due** as claimed by the secured creditor or as determined by the DRT, whichever is less. The DRAT may, for reasons recorded in writing, reduce the amount to **not less than twenty-five per cent**.

The deposit is a condition for the appeal to be entertained. This is the reduced form of the seventy-five per cent condition that the Supreme Court struck down in *Mardia Chemicals* (2004).

## Points to remember

- The amount is calculated on the debt claimed in the notice, not on the value of the property.
- A request to reduce the deposit should be made along with the appeal, supported by a financial statement.
- A secured creditor appealing does not need to make the deposit.
- Third parties, such as purchasers or tenants, should confirm whether the deposit condition applies to them in their capacity.

## Further remedy

The DRAT's order can be challenged only through the writ jurisdiction of the High Court under Articles 226 and 227, which is narrow. That makes the record built before the DRT and DRAT especially important.
