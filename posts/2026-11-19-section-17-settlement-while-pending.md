A settlement during a Section 17 application is possible, because nothing in the SARFAESI Act 2002 stops a borrower and a secured creditor from agreeing terms while the Debts Recovery Tribunal (DRT) case is pending. What matters is that the agreement is clear, in writing, and reflected in the Tribunal's records so that both the dues and the pending case are closed together.

## Can you settle while the Section 17 application is pending?

Yes. The application is a challenge to a measure taken under Section 13(4), but the underlying dispute is still a money dispute between a borrower and a lender. Banks and borrowers often talk to each other even after the Tribunal has issued notice.

Talking does not pause the case. Unless the Tribunal passes an order, the bank's measures and the hearing dates carry on as before. Attend each hearing and tell the Tribunal if talks are under way, so that dates are not lost.

## How does a settlement usually begin?

Most settlements start with a written proposal from the borrower, or an invitation from the bank. A proposal is only an offer. It does not bind the bank until the bank accepts it in writing, and it does not bind you until you accept the bank's terms.

Our article on [one-time settlement with a bank during SARFAESI proceedings](/blog/2026-10-09-one-time-settlement-sarfaesi) explains how to prepare an offer. For the legal background on settlement and redemption, see [one-time settlement and redemption](/one-time-settlement-redemption).

## What should the settlement record in writing?

Do not rely on a phone call or an informal message. The bank's settlement letter or the signed agreement should cover:

- The exact sum payable, the dates or instalments, and the account to which it is paid.
- What happens to the possession or sale steps already taken, and whether any sale is to be stopped.
- Treatment of interest and charges, if the bank has agreed to any waiver.
- Whether the bank will release the security, return title documents and issue a no-dues certificate after payment.
- What happens if you miss a payment, including whether the bank can revive its measures.
- That the Section 17 application will be withdrawn or disposed of on the agreed terms.

Do not assume that any of these points follows automatically. If a term is not in the document, treat it as not agreed.

## How is the DRT case closed?

Paying the agreed amount does not by itself end the case. The pending application has to be brought to a close in a way the Tribunal records.

Commonly this is done by a joint memo or a recorded statement of both sides, placed before the Tribunal, saying that the matter has been settled and stating the terms or the fact of settlement. The Tribunal then disposes of the application. Check the current DRT rules and the practice of the Tribunal in your matter on the form and timing.

Be careful about timing. If the application is withdrawn before the bank has performed its side, you may lose the protection the pending case gave you. If it is kept pending after payment, the case may run on without purpose. A sensible order is usually to agree the terms, have them recorded, and then follow the agreed sequence of payment and withdrawal.

## What if the settlement fails?

If the settlement is not honoured, the position depends on the terms signed. A clause that revives the bank's rights on default, or that leaves the borrower free to pursue the application, will matter greatly.

Remember that the SARFAESI Act and the Recovery of Debts and Bankruptcy Act 1993 operate together, as recognised in *Transcore v. UOI* (2008) 1 SCC 125. A failed settlement can therefore leave more than one recovery route open to the lender. For a wider view of the routes, see our article on [grounds to challenge SARFAESI action](/blog/2026-11-17-section-17-grounds-to-challenge), which shows what a borrower may still raise if the case continues.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have to withdraw my Section 17 application to settle?
Not necessarily. Some settlements are recorded by consent and the application is disposed of on those terms. What the Tribunal records, and when, depends on the agreement and on the Tribunal's practice.

### Can the bank continue the sale while we negotiate?
Unless there is a Tribunal order or a written agreement to hold back, the bank may continue with its steps. If you want the sale paused, get that commitment in writing or raise it before the Tribunal.

### Is a settlement offer an admission of liability?
It can be read that way, so draft it carefully and say that it is made without prejudice to your rights in the Section 17 application. Take advice on the wording before sending it.
