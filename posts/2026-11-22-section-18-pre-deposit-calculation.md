The Section 18 pre-deposit calculation starts from the debt, not the property: a borrower who appeals to the DRAT must deposit fifty per cent of the debt due, taking the lower of the amount claimed by the secured creditor and the amount determined by the DRT. The DRAT can reduce this for recorded reasons, but not below twenty-five per cent.

## What does Section 18 require?

Section 18(1) of the SARFAESI Act 2002 allows an appeal to the Debts Recovery Appellate Tribunal against an order of the DRT under Section 17. Where the appellant is a borrower, the appeal is not entertained unless the deposit is made. The Supreme Court in *Mardia Chemicals v. UOI* (2004) 4 SCC 311 struck down the earlier, higher deposit condition, and the present provision is the result. Check the current text of Section 18 before relying on any detail here.

## How is the fifty per cent worked out?

Follow these steps:

1. Find the debt due as claimed by the secured creditor in its notice and records.
2. Find the debt due as determined by the DRT, if the DRT has determined an amount.
3. Take the lower of the two figures.
4. Calculate fifty per cent of that lower figure.

The base is the debt, so the value of the property or the reserve price does not enter the calculation. Amounts already paid into the loan account may affect the debt figure, so ask for an updated statement of account.

## A simple worked example

The figures below are only for illustration. Suppose the bank claims Rs. 80 lakh. The DRT determines the debt due at Rs. 70 lakh. The lower figure is Rs. 70 lakh, so the deposit at the full rate is Rs. 35 lakh.

If the DRAT allows a reduction to the lowest permitted level, twenty-five per cent of Rs. 70 lakh is Rs. 17.5 lakh. If the DRT has not determined any figure, the claim in the secured creditor's papers is the figure to work from.

Where the bank's claim is the lower figure, that claim is used. A claim that was reduced during the proceedings should be shown clearly to the DRAT.

## Can the pre-deposit be reduced?

The DRAT may reduce the amount for reasons recorded in writing. The borrower should apply along with the appeal and support the request with a statement of means and the reasons why the full amount cannot be met. Do not assume a reduction will be allowed, and plan funds for the full amount in case it is not. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is the pre-deposit calculated on the property value?
No. It is a percentage of the debt due, not of the value of the secured asset or the sale price.

### Does a secured creditor have to make the deposit?
The condition applies to a borrower who appeals. A secured creditor appealing against a DRT order is not the person the deposit condition is aimed at, but check the current text of Section 18.

### Can I pay the deposit in instalments?
The Act speaks of a deposit as a condition of the appeal being entertained. Any indulgence on timing is for the DRAT to decide, so do not rely on it without an order.
