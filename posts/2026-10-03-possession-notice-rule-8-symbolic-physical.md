When a borrower does not pay within the 60 days given in a Section 13(2) notice, the bank may take possession of the secured asset under Section 13(4) of the SARFAESI Act 2002. For immovable property, the procedure is set out in **Rule 8** of the Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules 2002. Borrowers often hear the terms "symbolic possession" and "physical possession" without knowing what they mean.

## Symbolic possession

Symbolic possession is usually the first step. The authorised officer of the bank issues a possession notice, which is published in newspapers and affixed on the property or at a conspicuous place. The notice tells the public that the bank has taken possession and that dealing with the property is subject to the bank's charge. Often the borrower or occupant is **still living in or using the property** at this stage.

## Physical possession

Physical possession means the bank actually takes control of the property, for example by removing the occupant or securing the premises. If the borrower or occupant does not hand over the property, the bank may seek help under Section 14, where the Chief Metropolitan Magistrate or District Magistrate can assist in taking possession. Section 14 is covered in a separate post on this blog.

## Why Rule 8 compliance matters

The Supreme Court in *Mathew Varghese v. M. Amritha Kumar* (2014) 5 SCC 610 stressed that Rules 8 and 9 must be complied with. Borrowers should therefore check:

1. Was a proper possession notice issued in the form the Rules require?
2. Was it published and served as the Rules provide?
3. Does the notice correctly describe the property and the dues?
4. Was the valuation and sale process started only after the required steps?

Check the current text of Rule 8 for the exact steps and timelines.

## What can a borrower do?

- Read the possession notice carefully and note its date.
- If the borrower believes the bank has not followed the law, the remedy is a **Section 17 application** to the Debts Recovery Tribunal, which has a limit of 45 days from the date of the measure.
- Keep records of how and when the notice was served or affixed.
- Do not sign handover papers or remove goods without understanding the consequences.

## Getting advice

Possession steps move quickly, and the facts of each case differ. Getting the notice and loan documents reviewed early makes it easier to decide whether and how to respond.
