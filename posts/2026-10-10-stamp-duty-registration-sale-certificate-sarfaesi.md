Winning a bank auction is only part of the process. For an immovable property sold under the SARFAESI Act 2002, the buyer needs a proper **sale certificate**, and in many cases stamp duty and registration are needed to complete the title.

## What is a sale certificate?

Once the sale is confirmed and the price is paid, the secured creditor issues a sale certificate to the purchaser under the Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules 2002. This document is the buyer's primary evidence that the property was sold and transferred. Check the current text of **Rule 9** and the related Rules for the form and steps.

## Why stamp duty matters

A sale certificate transferring immovable property is generally treated as an instrument that attracts **stamp duty**. The rate and the rules differ from state to state, and they change from time to time, so the **current stamp law of the relevant state** must be checked. An under-stamped document can cause trouble later, for example when the buyer wants to mutate the property in the records or sell it.

## Why registration matters

Under general property law, a document that transfers rights in immovable property above a certain value usually requires registration under the Registration Act 1908. A buyer should confirm with a local advocate or the sub-registrar's office how a sale certificate is to be registered in that state, and within what time.

## A buyer's checklist

1. **Confirm the sale certificate** is in the correct form and describes the property accurately.
2. **Pay the stamp duty** as per the state's rules and keep the receipt.
3. **Register the certificate** at the proper sub-registrar's office if required.
4. **Apply for mutation** in revenue or municipal records.
5. **Update utility and tax records** in the buyer's name.
6. **Take possession** and keep a record of the handover.

## Points to remember

- A bank sale does not remove every problem. Dues such as property tax or society charges may remain, so check them before bidding.
- The Supreme Court in *Mathew Varghese v. M. Amritha Kumar* (2014) 5 SCC 610 stressed the importance of complying with Rules 8 and 9 in the sale process, which is why a buyer should keep all the sale notices and receipts.
- If possession is not handed over, the buyer may need to seek help under Section 14 or approach the appropriate forum.

## Getting advice

Stamp duty and registration rules vary by state and by the nature of the property. A local review of the sale certificate and the title papers helps avoid problems later.
