The documents for a Section 17 application are mainly the loan papers, every notice the bank issued, your correspondence with it, and proof of the date on which you received each notice. A complete, well-arranged set helps the Debts Recovery Tribunal (DRT) see exactly which measure under Section 13(4) you are challenging and why.

## Why the document set matters

A Section 17 application is decided largely on paper. The Tribunal looks at what the bank did, when it did it, and whether the steps required by the SARFAESI Act 2002 and the Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules 2002 were followed. Gaps in your papers can leave a good ground unproven.

Start collecting early, because the time limit for filing runs from the measure. Our article on the [Section 17 application and its time limit](/blog/2026-10-02-section-17-application-drt) explains why waiting is risky.

## Loan and security documents

These show what the bank claims and on what basis:

- The sanction letter and loan agreement, with all amendments or restructuring letters.
- The mortgage deed, hypothecation deed or other security documents, and any guarantee deeds.
- Statements of the loan account from disbursement to date.
- Any letters showing how the account was classified as a non-performing asset.

Compare the statements with your own payment records. Our note on [checking the loan account before replying to a notice](/blog/2026-10-20-checking-loan-account-before-replying-sarfaesi-notice) shows what to look for.

## Notices and bank correspondence

Collect every step the bank took in sequence:

1. The Section 13(2) demand notice, with the envelope or courier receipt that proves when you received it.
2. Your reply or objections, and the bank's response giving reasons. *Mardia Chemicals v. UOI* (2004) 4 SCC 311 recognises that a borrower can object and the creditor must reply with reasons.
3. The possession notice, sale notice and any publication in newspapers or online.
4. Any order or notice relating to the measure you are challenging.

Keep originals safe and file clear copies. Note the date of receipt on each.

## Documents specific to the measure challenged

What else you need depends on the measure. For a possession challenge, add proof of your occupation and any panchnama or possession record. For a sale, add the sale notice, valuation details if you have them, and details of the reserve price. Rules 8 and 9 must be complied with, as *Mathew Varghese v. M. Amritha Kumar* (2014) 5 SCC 610 stresses, so documents showing the dates and contents of those steps are valuable.

## Documents for the application itself

Besides the evidence, prepare:

- Identity and address proof of the applicant, and authority papers if a company or firm applies.
- A chronology of events in date order.
- An index and page-numbered paper book.
- Proof of court fee and any other filing requirement. Check the current rules for the amount and format.

## Frequently asked questions

### What if I do not have all the documents?
File within time with what you have and say what is missing. You can often ask the bank to produce its own records, so note which documents are in its hands.

### Should I attach the loan agreement even if I do not challenge it?
Yes, the Tribunal usually needs the loan and security documents to understand the dispute. Attach the relevant parts and keep the full set ready.

### Do I need to attach originals?
Generally copies are filed with the originals kept ready for inspection. Check the current filing practice of the DRT concerned.
