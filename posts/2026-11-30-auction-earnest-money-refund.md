A SARFAESI auction EMD refund is the return of the earnest money a bidder pays to take part in a bank auction. As a rule, a bidder who does not win has the deposit returned, while the successful bidder's deposit is adjusted against the price. A winning bidder who defaults on payment risks losing it. This article explains what earnest money is and the situations in which it comes back.

## What is earnest money in a bank auction?

Earnest money, often called EMD, is a deposit a bidder pays before bidding. It shows that the bidder is serious and gives the secured creditor some protection if a bidder withdraws after the sale. The Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules 2002 deal with the sale of immovable secured assets, and the sale notice sets out the terms on which bids are invited, including the deposit.

The sale notice states the amount, so read it carefully, and check the current text of Rules 8 and 9 for what the Rules themselves say about deposits.

## When is the EMD refunded?

The common situations are these:

- The bidder is not the highest bidder, so the sale goes to someone else.
- The sale is cancelled or called off by the secured creditor before it is concluded.
- The bidder is declared the purchaser, pays in full, and the deposit is adjusted as part of the price, so nothing is refunded separately.

Where the bidder fails to win, the bank should return the deposit without conditions that are not in the sale notice. The notice usually states the mode and time for the return, so keep a copy of it.

## When can the EMD be forfeited?

The Rules provide that if the purchaser does not pay the price within the time allowed, the deposit may be forfeited to the secured creditor and the asset may be sold again. Forfeiture follows from the purchaser's own default. It is different from a case where the bank cancels the sale or where the bidder simply lost.

A purchaser who thinks the default was caused by a defect in the sale, for example an incomplete notice or an undisclosed encumbrance, should raise it in writing at once. Whether the deposit can be recovered depends on the facts and on the forum, and a stay or order from the tribunal may be needed.

## What should a bidder do to protect the deposit?

1. Read the sale notice fully and note the last date for the deposit and the account to which it is paid.
2. Pay only through the mode the notice permits and keep the proof of payment.
3. Check the title and encumbrances before bidding, as discussed in our [buying at a SARFAESI auction checklist](/blog/2026-10-06-buying-at-sarfaesi-auction-checklist).
4. Note the payment deadlines that follow the bid. The stages are laid out in the [SARFAESI auction timeline](/blog/2026-11-28-sarfaesi-auction-timeline).
5. If the refund is delayed, ask the authorised officer in writing and keep a record of the request.

For online sales, see our note on [e-auction bidding pitfalls](/blog/2026-10-18-e-auction-bidding-pitfalls-sarfaesi).

## Can a refund be disputed before the tribunal?

Where a bank refuses to return a deposit that should be returned, or a sale is challenged, the matter may be taken up in a proceeding under Section 17 before the Debts Recovery Tribunal. In Mathew Varghese v. M. Amritha Kumar (2014) 5 SCC 610, the Supreme Court stressed that these Rules are to be complied with. Our page on [SARFAESI auction disputes](/sarfaesi-auction-disputes) sets out the common grounds. The time to file is fixed by the Act, so check the current text of Section 17.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I get my EMD back if I lose the auction?
Generally yes. A bidder who is not the highest bidder should have the deposit returned, and the sale notice usually states how and when. If it is delayed, write to the authorised officer and keep a copy.

### Can the bank keep my EMD if I do not pay the balance?
The Rules allow forfeiture of the deposit if the purchaser fails to pay within the permitted time. Check the current text of Rule 9 for the periods and the consequences.

### Is the EMD refunded if the auction is cancelled?
Where the secured creditor cancels or does not conclude the sale, the deposit is ordinarily returned to the bidders. Look at the terms of the sale notice, since they govern the bid.
