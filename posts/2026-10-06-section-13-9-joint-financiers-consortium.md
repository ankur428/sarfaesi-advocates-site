Large borrowers often take finance from more than one lender, either through a consortium or a multiple banking arrangement. In such cases, **Section 13(9) of the SARFAESI Act 2002** lays down how secured creditors may enforce their rights.

## What Section 13(9) says in broad terms

Where a borrower has been financed by more than one secured creditor, or by a joint financing arrangement, **no single secured creditor can freely enforce on its own**. The Act requires the agreement of secured creditors representing a prescribed share, by value, of the amount outstanding. The exact proportion and conditions have been amended over the years, so **check the current text of the Act**.

## Why this provision exists

The idea is to avoid a race among lenders to grab the same asset. A collective decision by the creditors supports an orderly process, and it may help to protect the value of the security for everyone.

## Why it matters to a borrower

If a bank issues a Section 13(2) notice or takes a Section 13(4) measure in a multi-lender case, a borrower can ask:

1. Is the borrower financed by more than one secured creditor?
2. Is there a consortium or joint lenders' arrangement?
3. Did the creditors representing the required share in value agree to the enforcement?
4. Is the bank relying on a decision of the lenders' forum, and can the borrower see the relevant record?

If the answers suggest that the provision was not followed, the point can be raised in the reply to the notice and, where needed, in a Section 17 application before the Debts Recovery Tribunal within the time limit.

## Related points

- The Supreme Court in *Mardia Chemicals v. Union of India* (2004) 4 SCC 311 recognised that a borrower can object and that the creditor must reply with reasons.
- Priority among secured creditors is a separate subject, which depends on the security documents and, where applicable, registration with the Central Registry.
- Lenders may also use the RDB Act route, and in *Transcore v. Union of India* (2008) 1 SCC 125, the Supreme Court held that both laws operate together.

## Practical tips for borrowers

- Keep a list of all lenders, facilities and security documents.
- Ask each lender for a statement of account.
- Keep minutes or correspondence of any lenders' meetings.
- Respond to every notice in writing and diarise the dates.

## Getting advice

Multi-lender cases involve many documents and varying facts. Having the arrangement reviewed early helps in understanding what a notice really requires.
