Sale of a secured immovable asset is governed by **Rules 8 and 9 of the Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules, 2002**. Each step is a potential ground for challenge.

## Key safeguards

- **Valuation and reserve price.** The authorised officer must obtain a valuation and fix a reserve price. A sale below the reserve price is not permitted.
- **Notice of sale.** A notice of at least **thirty days** must be given to the borrower and guarantors, and published in newspapers. It should describe the asset, encumbrances known to the creditor, the reserve price, and the time and place of the sale.
- **Mode of sale.** Public auction, tender, or, in permitted cases, private treaty, with the applicable conditions.
- **Earnest money and payment.** The highest bidder ordinarily deposits twenty-five per cent on the sale being knocked down, and the balance is due within fifteen days unless otherwise agreed in writing.

## Grounds to challenge a sale

1. The notice was not served on the borrower or on all guarantors.
2. The period of notice was short of thirty days.
3. There was no proper valuation, or the valuation is stale or grossly low.
4. Material encumbrances or facts were not disclosed.
5. The auction was not conducted transparently, for example there was collusion or an unreasonable bidder eligibility condition.

## Approach of the courts

The Supreme Court in *Mathew Varghese v. M. Amritha Kumar* (2014) 5 SCC 610 held that the Rules must be strictly followed, and that the borrower's right to notice and to redeem is an important safeguard. Even so, courts are reluctant to disturb a completed auction unless a serious irregularity is shown, so a challenge should be brought before the sale is confirmed.
