Borrowers frequently ask whether they can file a writ petition in the High Court against a SARFAESI notice or auction. The short answer is: **only in limited cases**.

## The rule of alternative remedy

The Act provides a full remedy through Section 17 before the DRT and Section 18 before the DRAT. In *United Bank of India v. Satyawati Tondon* (2010) 8 SCC 110, the Supreme Court cautioned High Courts against entertaining writ petitions that bypass these remedies, and said the practice should be exceptional.

## Situations in which a writ may still be considered

- The action is **wholly without jurisdiction**, for example the Act does not apply to the asset or the lender is not a secured creditor.
- There is a **violation of fundamental rights or natural justice** that the Tribunal cannot address.
- The Tribunal is **not functioning**, or a remedy is illusory.
- There is a clear breach of **statutory mandate** that goes to the root of the action.

These are exceptions, and the court may still relegate the petitioner to the DRT.

## Practical advice

1. Keep the 45-day period under Section 17 in mind. Filing a writ does not stop the clock.
2. If time is running out, file in the DRT and, if needed, seek liberty to raise jurisdictional points there.
3. Frame the writ petition on legal grounds rather than disputed accounts.

Choosing the right forum on day one saves months and avoids adverse costs.
