Many banks now sell secured properties through **online e-auctions** on authorised portals. The idea is to reach more bidders and make the process more transparent. This guide explains how an e-auction generally works and the pitfalls to avoid.

## The legal frame

The sale of immovable property under SARFAESI is governed by the Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules 2002, including a notice of sale, a reserve price and a stated period before the sale. **Check the current text of Rules 8 and 9** for the details. The Supreme Court in *Mathew Varghese v. M. Amritha Kumar* (2014) 5 SCC 610 stressed that these Rules must be complied with.

## How an e-auction usually works

1. **Notice:** the bank publishes the sale notice, giving the property description, reserve price, earnest money and inspection dates.
2. **Registration:** bidders register on the auction portal and complete KYC.
3. **Earnest money deposit (EMD):** bidders pay the EMD as stated in the notice.
4. **Bidding:** bidders place online bids within the auction window. Many portals extend the time if bids arrive near the end, as the notice usually explains.
5. **Result:** the highest bidder above the reserve price may be declared successful, subject to the bank's confirmation as the Rules provide.
6. **Payment:** the balance is paid within the time given in the Rules and the notice.

## Common pitfalls for bidders

- **Not reading the notice** fully, including "as is where is" terms.
- **Skipping title checks** and a search of encumbrances and dues.
- **Missing the EMD or KYC deadline.**
- **Bidding without inspecting** the property.
- **Ignoring possession risk**, as the bank may not hand over vacant possession immediately.
- **Delay in paying the balance**, which can lead to forfeiture as the notice may state.

## Pitfalls for borrowers

- **Not checking the sale notice** for defects in time, valuation or description.
- **Waiting until after the auction** to challenge the process.
- **Missing the 45-day limit** for a Section 17 application before the Tribunal.
- **Overlooking redemption**, since the borrower may be able to clear dues before the sale as Section 13(8) provides.

## Getting advice

Auction terms vary from one notice to another. Reviewing the notice and the property papers before bidding or before challenging a sale helps avoid costly errors.
