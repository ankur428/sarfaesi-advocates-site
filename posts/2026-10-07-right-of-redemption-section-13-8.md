Section 13(8) protects the borrower's right to **redeem** the secured asset by paying the secured creditor's dues in full.

## The deadline

Earlier, the right could be exercised at any time before the date fixed for sale. Since the 2016 amendment, the borrower may tender all dues, together with costs, charges and expenses incurred by the secured creditor, **at any time before the date of publication of the notice for public auction or for inviting quotations, tender from the public or private treaty**.

After that date, the right to redeem under this provision is no longer available.

## Why it matters

- The date on which the auction notice is first published becomes the practical deadline.
- The amount to be tendered includes the costs of enforcement, so it grows as the process advances.
- Settlements, part-payments and one-time settlements should be concluded, and the money paid, before the notice appears.

## Judicial approach

In *Mathew Varghese v. M. Amritha Kumar* (2014) 5 SCC 610, the Supreme Court stressed that the Rules on notice and sale are integral to the borrower's ability to redeem, and that non-compliance can invalidate the sale.

## What to do

If you receive a possession notice, ask the creditor for a statement of dues, arrange the funds, and tender the amount in writing while retaining proof. Do not wait for the auction date.
