A no dues certificate after settlement is the bank's written confirmation that nothing more is owed on the loan account. Paying the last instalment of a settlement does not finish the job: the borrower still needs that certificate and the original title documents back. This article explains what to ask for and how to keep a record.

## What is a no dues certificate?

A no dues certificate is a letter or certificate from the bank stating that the account has been closed after receipt of the agreed amount and that no amount remains payable under it. It is the document you will rely on if a question about the loan ever comes up again, for example with a buyer, a new lender or a credit information company.

Do not treat an acknowledgement of payment as the same thing. A receipt shows that money came in; the certificate shows that the account is closed on the terms agreed.

## Which documents should you ask the bank for?

Once the settlement is fully paid in line with the sanction letter, ask in writing for:

- the no dues certificate or account closure letter;
- the original title deeds and other documents deposited with the bank as security;
- a release of the security interest, and a note that any charge registered in the bank's favour has been or will be satisfied;
- the return of any post-dated cheques, guarantees or other instruments held for the account.

Our post on the [settlement letter and what to insist on in writing](/blog/2026-12-07-settlement-letter-in-writing) explains why the sanction letter itself should say that these will be given.

## How do you get the title documents back?

Make a short written request to the branch or the officer handling the account. Quote the account number, the date and amount of the final payment, and list the documents you expect to receive. Ask the bank to confirm in writing a date for handing them over.

When you collect the documents, check them against the list you gave when the loan was taken. Sign a receipt that records what was returned, and note any item that is missing. If a document is lost, raise it with the bank straight away in writing.

## What about charges recorded against the property?

A mortgage or charge may be recorded with a registering authority. Ask the bank what it will file to show the charge has been satisfied, and keep copies. What each registry requires differs, so check the procedure that applies to your property.

If the loan was classified as a non-performing account, ask the bank to update its records and its reporting accordingly. Whether and how this changes your credit history depends on the bank's reporting, so keep the certificate to hand.

## What if the bank delays?

Start with a written reminder that refers to the sanction letter and your proof of payment. Escalate to the bank's grievance channel if there is no reply. If the settlement was reached while a challenge was pending, check that the proceedings have been closed as agreed; our post on [settling while a Section 17 application is pending](/blog/2026-11-19-section-17-settlement-while-pending) covers this. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is a payment receipt enough after a one time settlement?
No. A receipt only proves that money was paid. Ask for a no dues certificate or account closure letter that states the account is closed and nothing remains payable.

### How long does the bank take to return the title documents?
Check the sanction letter and the bank's own policy, and check the current regulatory directions, for any stated period. Ask for a written date when you make your request.

### What if the bank says some dues are still pending after settlement?
Compare the demand with the sanction letter and your proof of payment. If the settlement was meant to be full and final, point that out in writing and ask the bank to confirm the account is closed.
