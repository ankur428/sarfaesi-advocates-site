A bank cannot send a Section 13(2) demand notice under the SARFAESI Act 2002 just because a payment is late. The account must first be classified as a **non-performing asset (NPA)**. Understanding NPA classification helps a borrower test whether a notice was issued on a proper footing.

## What is an NPA?

An NPA is a loan account that has stopped performing as per the classification norms of the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) or the relevant regulator. Under the usual RBI norms, an account becomes an NPA when interest or instalments remain overdue beyond a stated period, commonly 90 days. Since norms are revised from time to time, **check the current RBI circulars** for the exact test that applies to your type of loan.

## Why NPA status matters for Section 13(2)

Section 13(2) allows a secured creditor to call upon the borrower to discharge the liability in full within **60 days** of the notice. The Act links this notice to the account being classified as a non-performing asset. If the account was not an NPA when the notice was issued, the borrower has a basis to question the notice.

## What a borrower should check in the notice

- **The NPA date** stated in the notice, and whether it matches the statement of account.
- **The amount claimed**, split into principal, interest and charges.
- **The secured assets** described, and whether they match the mortgage or hypothecation documents.
- **The 60-day period** and the date from which it runs.
- **The authority** of the person who signed the notice.

## Responding to the notice

The Supreme Court in *Mardia Chemicals v. Union of India* (2004) 4 SCC 311 recognised that a borrower can object to the demand and that the creditor must reply with reasons. In practice this means:

1. Send a written reply within the notice period, pointing to specific errors.
2. Ask for a full statement of account and the NPA classification details.
3. Keep proof of every payment made.
4. Diarise the dates, because later remedies carry strict time limits.

If the bank takes a measure under Section 13(4) after your reply, the remedy is a Section 17 application before the Debts Recovery Tribunal, not usually a writ petition, as noted in *United Bank of India v. Satyawati Tondon* (2010) 8 SCC 110.

## Getting advice

Whether the NPA classification and notice are in order depends on your loan agreement and account records. Having the documents reviewed soon after the notice arrives leaves more room to respond within time.
