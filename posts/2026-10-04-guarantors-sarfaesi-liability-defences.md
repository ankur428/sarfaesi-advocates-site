Many people sign as guarantor for a relative's or a company's loan without expecting trouble. If the loan turns bad, the guarantor may receive a notice under the SARFAESI Act 2002. This post explains in general terms how a guarantor's position works.

## How guarantor liability works

Under general contract law, the liability of a guarantor is usually **co-extensive with that of the principal borrower**, unless the guarantee document says otherwise. In other words, the bank may ask the guarantor to pay the same dues. The Act's definition of "borrower" is wide and, in many situations, includes a person who has given a guarantee or created a mortgage or pledge as security. **Check the current text of the Act** for the exact definition.

## Notices a guarantor may receive

A guarantor may be sent a Section 13(2) notice calling for repayment within **60 days**. If the guarantor has also mortgaged property as security, the bank's measures under Section 13(4) can extend to that property. A guarantor who gave only a personal guarantee, with no property security, presents a different position, and the bank may need to rely on other routes such as an application under the Recovery of Debts Act 1993.

## Points a guarantor should check

1. **The guarantee deed:** its terms, amount, period and any limits on liability.
2. **Changes to the loan:** whether the loan was varied, enhanced or restructured without the guarantor's consent.
3. **The notice:** whether it names the guarantor, states the amount correctly and describes the secured asset accurately.
4. **The statement of account:** whether the dues are properly worked out.
5. **Security held:** whether the bank has other security it should look to first, as per the documents.

Whether any of these points amounts to a defence depends on the documents and the facts, so it should not be assumed.

## Remedies

A guarantor who disagrees with a bank's action can raise objections in reply to the notice. If a Section 13(4) measure is taken, a Section 17 application before the Debts Recovery Tribunal is available, within the time limit in the Act. The Supreme Court in *Transcore v. Union of India* (2008) 1 SCC 125 held that SARFAESI and the RDB Act operate together, so a guarantor may face both processes.

## Practical tips

- Keep a copy of every document you signed.
- Reply to every bank notice in writing.
- Do not assume that signing as guarantor was a mere formality.

## Getting advice

A guarantor's rights turn on the exact wording of the guarantee and loan papers. A prompt review of the documents helps in deciding the next step.
