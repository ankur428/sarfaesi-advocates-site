When a bank starts action under the SARFAESI Act, the notice usually arrives with a deadline. Time spent searching for papers is time lost. Gathering your records early helps you read the notice properly, reply with specifics and, if needed, explain your position before the Tribunal. This article lists the papers that are commonly useful.

## Loan and security papers

- The sanction letter and all loan agreements, including any later amendments.
- Documents creating the security, such as the mortgage deed, hypothecation deed or a record of deposit of title deeds.
- Guarantee deeds, if you signed as guarantor.
- Any restructuring, rescheduling or settlement letters exchanged with the bank.

These show what the bank is entitled to enforce and against which asset. The notice under Section 13(2) must state the amount payable and the secured assets the bank intends to enforce, so the two sets of papers should match.

## Payment records

- Loan account statements for the full life of the account.
- Receipts, bank entries and challans for every repayment you made.
- Interest rate change communications and charge breakdowns.

Compare the amount claimed in the notice with your own records. Differences in interest, charges or credits are worth noting in writing.

## Notices and correspondence

- The Section 13(2) demand notice, with the envelope, courier slip or email showing when you received it.
- Any earlier reminders, recall notices or letters about the account being classified as a non-performing asset.
- Possession notices, valuation reports, auction notices and sale notices, if they have been issued.
- Your own replies and proof that you sent them.

Dates matter. The date of the notice, the date of receipt and the dates on later notices help show whether the stages of the process were followed. The Supreme Court in *Mathew Varghese v. M. Amritha Kumar* (2014) 5 SCC 610 stressed that the Rules, including Rules 8 and 9 of the Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules, 2002, must be complied with.

## Identity and ownership papers

- Title documents and proof of your ownership or occupation of the property.
- Lease or tenancy papers if the property is occupied by others.
- Proof of address, so notices can be shown to have reached, or not reached, the correct place.

## How to organise them

Make a copy of everything. Arrange the papers by date, note when each notice arrived, and write a short timeline of key events. Keep originals safe and work from copies.

## Why this helps

A borrower can raise objections to a Section 13(2) notice, and in *Mardia Chemicals v. Union of India* (2004) 4 SCC 311 the Supreme Court recognised that the creditor must reply with reasons. A reply is stronger when it points to specific documents instead of general complaints. If you later approach the Tribunal under Section 17, an organised file also saves time.

Every case turns on its own facts, and limits for reply or challenge are short. Check the current text of the Act and the Rules, and consider taking legal advice on your documents early.
