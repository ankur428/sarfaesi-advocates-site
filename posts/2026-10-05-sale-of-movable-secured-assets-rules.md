SARFAESI action is not limited to land and buildings. A secured creditor can also enforce security over **movable assets** such as machinery, stock, vehicles or receivables that were hypothecated or pledged. The Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules 2002 treat movable and immovable assets separately, so the steps differ.

## Where the Rules deal with movables

In broad terms, **Rule 7** deals with taking possession of movable secured assets, while **Rule 8** and **Rule 9** deal mainly with immovable property. Check the current text of the Rules for the exact procedure, because the steps for movables, including inventory, custody and sale, are set out there.

## Typical steps for movables

1. A Section 13(2) notice calls for repayment within 60 days.
2. If the borrower does not pay, the bank may take possession of the movable asset under Section 13(4).
3. The bank prepares an inventory of what it has taken.
4. The asset is valued and then sold, for example by inviting quotations or tenders, public auction or private treaty as the Rules permit.
5. The sale proceeds are applied as the Act provides.

## Practical issues with movables

- **Identification:** stock and machinery can change over time, so the description in the security documents matters.
- **Custody:** assets taken by the bank must be kept safely and an inventory made.
- **Valuation:** movable assets can lose value quickly, so a fair valuation is important.
- **Third-party rights:** goods on the premises may belong to someone else, such as a supplier or a lessor.

The Supreme Court in *Harshad Govardhan Sondagar v. International Asset Reconstruction Co.* (2014) 6 SCC 1 dealt with the rights of lessees, which can matter where leased equipment or premises are involved.

## What a borrower can do

- Compare the inventory prepared by the bank with the actual assets.
- Object in writing if goods not covered by the security have been taken.
- If the process does not follow the Act or the Rules, file a Section 17 application before the Debts Recovery Tribunal, within the time limit in the Act.
- Where a possession step has been taken, ask for the valuation and sale details.

## Getting advice

Movable asset cases depend heavily on documents such as hypothecation deeds and stock statements. A prompt review helps in deciding how to respond.
