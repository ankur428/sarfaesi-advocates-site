A home is usually a family's biggest asset, so a SARFAESI notice for a home loan default is stressful. The process has stages, and there are choices at each stage. This post lists the main options in general terms.

## Stage 1: Before a notice

When EMIs are missed, speak to the bank early. Possible outcomes include a **revised repayment plan** or a **restructuring** where the bank's policy allows. The decision is the bank's, but an early, honest request with documents is better than silence.

## Stage 2: After the Section 13(2) notice

The notice gives **60 days** to clear the dues. During this period you can:

1. **Pay the dues** claimed, or an agreed part, and ask for the account to be regularised.
2. **Send a written reply** pointing out errors in the amount, the security or the process. The Supreme Court in *Mardia Chemicals v. Union of India* (2004) 4 SCC 311 recognised that the creditor must respond with reasons.
3. **Ask for a settlement or restructuring proposal** in writing.

## Stage 3: After possession or auction steps

If the bank takes a measure under Section 13(4), such as a possession notice, you can:

- File a **Section 17 application** before the Debts Recovery Tribunal within 45 days, if you believe the process was defective.
- Ask the bank for a **one-time settlement**, as explained in an earlier post on this blog.
- Review the **auction notice and reserve price** for compliance with Rules 8 and 9, which the Supreme Court in *Mathew Varghese v. M. Amritha Kumar* (2014) 5 SCC 610 said must be followed.

## The right of redemption

A borrower may be able to clear the dues and redeem the property before the sale, as Section 13(8) provides. The post on this blog explains the time limit. Read the current text of the Act, because the position was clarified by case law and amendments.

## What not to do

- Do not ignore notices or refuse to accept them.
- Do not transfer or sell the property in a hurry without checking the bank's charge.
- Do not rely on a writ petition as the first step, because the Supreme Court in *United Bank of India v. Satyawati Tondon* (2010) 8 SCC 110 discouraged writs where a statutory remedy exists.
- Do not make promises to the bank that you cannot keep.

## Documents to gather

Loan agreement, sanction letter, statement of account, all bank notices, payment proofs and property papers.

## Getting advice

Each home loan case turns on its own papers and timelines. Taking advice soon after the first notice leaves the most options open.
