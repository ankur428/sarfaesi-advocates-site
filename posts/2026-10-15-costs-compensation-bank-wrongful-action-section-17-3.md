Banks are expected to follow the SARFAESI Act 2002 and the Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules 2002 when they act against a borrower. If they do not, the law provides a remedy. This post explains, in general terms, what the Debts Recovery Tribunal (DRT) can do under **Section 17(3)** and related provisions.

## What Section 17(3) allows

Where the Tribunal, after examining the facts and circumstances, concludes that a measure taken by the secured creditor does not comply with the Act and the Rules, it can **declare the measure invalid**. It may then direct that possession of the secured assets or management of the business be **restored** to the borrower, and pass such other orders as the Act permits. **Check the current text of Section 17** for the exact powers.

## Costs and compensation

The Act also contains provisions on costs and compensation where a borrower's challenge succeeds or where action was wrongly taken. The scope of these powers and when they apply should be read from the **current text of the Act**. In practice, the Tribunal's orders depend on what is proved and on the facts of each case, and no result should be assumed.

## What counts as non-compliance?

Examples of issues borrowers commonly raise include:

1. A **defective Section 13(2) notice**, such as wrong dues or a missing description of security.
2. **No reply to the borrower's objections**, which the Supreme Court in *Mardia Chemicals v. Union of India* (2004) 4 SCC 311 said the creditor must give with reasons.
3. **Possession or sale steps** that did not follow Rules 8 and 9, which the Supreme Court in *Mathew Varghese v. M. Amritha Kumar* (2014) 5 SCC 610 said must be complied with.
4. **Defects in valuation, publication or notice** of sale.

## Steps for a borrower

- File the Section 17 application **within 45 days** of the measure, as the Act provides.
- Attach copies of notices, replies, receipts and the account statement.
- State clearly which provision or Rule was not followed.
- Ask for the relief the Act allows, including restoration, if appropriate.
- Keep records of any loss caused, such as damaged goods or lost income, in case compensation is considered.

## What to avoid

The Supreme Court in *United Bank of India v. Satyawati Tondon* (2010) 8 SCC 110 discouraged writ petitions where the statutory remedy exists, so the Tribunal is usually the proper forum.

## Getting advice

Whether a bank's action was wrong depends on the documents and the exact sequence of steps. Getting the papers reviewed promptly helps in preserving the right to challenge in time.
