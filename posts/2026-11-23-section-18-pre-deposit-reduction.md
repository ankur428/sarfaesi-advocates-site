A borrower who wants to reduce pre-deposit at the DRAT must make a written request, give reasons, and support it with papers showing why the full amount cannot be met. Under Section 18 of the SARFAESI Act 2002, the Appellate Tribunal may reduce the deposit for reasons recorded in writing, but not below twenty-five per cent of the debt. Check the current text of the Act before relying on any detail here.

## What is the rule on reducing the Section 18 pre-deposit?

When a borrower appeals against a DRT order under Section 17, the appeal is not entertained unless fifty per cent of the debt due is deposited. The debt is taken as the lower of the amount claimed by the secured creditor and the amount determined by the DRT. The proviso allows the DRAT to reduce the amount, for reasons it records, to a level not below twenty-five per cent.

The power is discretionary. A reduction is something the Tribunal may allow, not something a borrower can claim as of right. Our article on [how the pre-deposit is worked out](/blog/2026-11-22-section-18-pre-deposit-calculation) shows the arithmetic with an example.

## How should the request be made?

The request is best made together with the appeal, so the Tribunal sees the reasons from the start. A separate application, supported by an affidavit, is the usual way to place the facts on record. Leaving the request to a later date risks the appeal being delayed or not entertained.

The application should state:

- the amount claimed by the secured creditor and the amount determined by the DRT;
- the amount the appellant has already paid or deposited, if any;
- the amount the appellant can arrange now, and the reason the balance cannot be paid;
- the specific percentage, not below the statutory floor, that the appellant asks the Tribunal to accept.

## What papers help the DRAT decide?

The Tribunal must give reasons, so the application should hand it facts to record. Useful papers include recent bank statements, income tax returns, a statement of assets and liabilities, and evidence of any other pending dues. Where the borrower is a business, financial statements and proof of its present trading position will matter.

Keep the papers accurate and consistent with what was said before the DRT. Statements that conflict with earlier filings weaken the request. Also avoid arguing the merits of the appeal in place of the reasons for reduction; the two are different questions.

## What if the reduction is refused?

Plan funds on the footing that the full amount may be required. If the DRAT declines the request, the appellant should be ready to deposit the amount directed within the time allowed, since the appeal depends on it. Any further remedy against such an order is a matter for legal advice on the facts. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can the DRAT reduce the pre-deposit below twenty-five per cent?
No. The Act sets twenty-five per cent of the debt as the lowest level the Tribunal can allow, and it must record its reasons for any reduction.

### Is a reduction automatic if the borrower is in financial difficulty?
No. The Tribunal decides on the facts and papers placed before it, so the borrower should explain the position with documents instead of assuming relief.

### Should I apply for reduction after filing the appeal?
It is safer to apply together with the appeal. Check the current rules of the Tribunal on how the application and the deposit are to be handled.
