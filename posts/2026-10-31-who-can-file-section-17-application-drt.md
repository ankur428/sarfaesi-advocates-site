When a bank takes a measure under Section 13(4) of the SARFAESI Act, many people assume only the named borrower can challenge it. In fact, Section 17 speaks of the borrower *or any other person* who is aggrieved by a measure taken by the secured creditor. Check the current text of the Act for the exact wording.

## Who may be an "aggrieved person"

The Act does not give an exhaustive list, so each case turns on its facts. People who commonly consider a Section 17 application include:

- **The borrower** whose account has been classified as a non-performing asset and whose secured asset is being enforced.
- **A guarantor or mortgagor** whose property or liability is affected by the bank's action.
- **A lessee or occupant** claiming a right in the secured asset. The Supreme Court considered lessees' rights in *Harshad Govardhan Sondagar v. IARC* (2014) 6 SCC 1.
- **A purchaser or other person** whose interest in the asset is affected by a measure the bank has taken.

Whether a particular person qualifies depends on the nature of their interest and the measure complained of. This is a question to examine with the documents in hand.

## What can be challenged

A Section 17 application is directed against measures taken under Section 13(4), such as taking possession of the secured asset or taking over its management. Because the Supreme Court in *Mardia Chemicals v. Union of India* (2004) 4 SCC 311 recognised that a borrower can object to a notice and the creditor must reply with reasons, the bank's reply to an objection is also an important part of the record.

## Why the forum matters

In *United Bank of India v. Satyawati Tondon* (2010) 8 SCC 110, the Supreme Court discouraged writ petitions where the Act provides an effective remedy. For most aggrieved persons, the Debts Recovery Tribunal is therefore the natural first forum. The Tribunal can examine whether the bank followed Rules 8 and 9 of the Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules, a point stressed in *Mathew Varghese v. M. Amritha Kumar* (2014) 5 SCC 610.

## What to keep ready

- The Section 13(2) notice and any later notices, with proof of delivery.
- Your written objections and the bank's reply, if any.
- The possession notice and any sale notice.
- Documents showing your own interest in the asset, such as a lease, mortgage deed or title papers.
- Loan statements and correspondence with the bank.

## Mind the time limit

A Section 17 application must be filed within the period the Act prescribes. Check the current text of the Act for that period and how it is counted, and do not wait until the last days to gather documents.

## In short

Section 17 is not reserved for the borrower alone. Any person genuinely aggrieved by a measure under Section 13(4) may consider approaching the Tribunal, subject to the facts and the time limit. This article is general information, not advice on any particular matter.
