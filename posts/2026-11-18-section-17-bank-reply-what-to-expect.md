A Section 17 bank reply is the written response the secured creditor files after the Debts Recovery Tribunal (DRT) issues notice on your application. It sets out the bank's version of the loan, the default and the steps it took under the SARFAESI Act 2002, and it is the document your own case will have to answer.

## What is a Section 17 bank reply?

Once a Section 17 application is admitted, the DRT calls on the bank to respond. The reply is the bank's chance to explain why it says its measure under Section 13(4) was lawful. It is usually filed through the bank's counsel, with documents attached.

The reply is not a judgment, and nothing in it is accepted merely because the bank says so. The Tribunal looks at whether the bank can show, from its papers, that it followed the Act and the Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules 2002. If you have not yet filed, see our guide on [how to file a Section 17 application](/blog/2026-11-15-section-17-application-step-by-step).

## What does the bank usually say?

Banks differ in style, but replies commonly cover the same ground:

- The loan sanction, the security documents and the borrower's liability.
- The date and basis on which the account became a non-performing asset.
- Service of the Section 13(2) demand notice and the borrower's reply, if any.
- The reasons given for rejecting the borrower's objections.
- The possession and sale steps taken, with dates.
- A statement of the amount said to be outstanding.

Many replies also raise preliminary points, such as delay in filing or a claim that the borrower should have made out a case on the merits. Whether such a point has substance depends on the facts and the current law, so check each one against your papers.

## How should you read the reply?

Read it against your own file, line by line. Mark each statement as agreed, disputed or unsupported. A date the bank gives for service of a notice should match the postal or courier record. An outstanding figure should match the loan statement.

The reply must also be consistent with what the bank wrote earlier. *Mardia Chemicals v. UOI* (2004) 4 SCC 311 recognises that a borrower can object and the creditor must reply with reasons. If the reply now gives reasons that were never communicated to you, that gap may be worth pointing out to the Tribunal.

Where the bank describes a possession or sale, compare its account with the requirements of Rules 8 and 9. *Mathew Varghese v. M. Amritha Kumar* (2014) 5 SCC 610 stresses that these Rules are to be complied with. Our article on [grounds to challenge SARFAESI action](/blog/2026-11-17-section-17-grounds-to-challenge) helps connect each defect to the right step.

## What can you do after the reply?

The borrower can usually respond in writing to the bank's reply, dealing only with the points that are actually disputed. Check the current procedural rules of the DRT for the form and timing of that response, since these are governed by the Tribunal's rules and its directions in your case.

A sensible approach is:

1. List each factual claim in the reply and note whether you can disprove it with a document.
2. Gather any paper the bank has not produced, such as proof of service or the valuation.
3. Prepare a short rejoinder that answers the new points without repeating the whole application.
4. Keep the dates of each hearing and each direction in one place.

A reply may also open a conversation about the dues. If you are considering that route, see our page on [one-time settlement and redemption](/one-time-settlement-redemption).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the bank bound to file a reply?
The Tribunal ordinarily directs the bank to respond, and the case proceeds on the pleadings and documents. Check the current DRT rules and the order passed in your matter for the exact directions.

### Can I file a response to the bank's reply?
Borrowers commonly file a rejoinder dealing with the points in dispute, subject to the Tribunal's procedure and permission. Check the current rules and any directions given at the hearing.

### Does the reply decide the application?
No. The reply is the bank's pleading and the documents attached to it are evidence the Tribunal weighs. The decision comes only after the Tribunal examines both sides.
