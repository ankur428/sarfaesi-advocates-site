A Section 17 application before the Debts Recovery Tribunal (DRT) is the main remedy against a bank's SARFAESI measures. Because the bank's process can move quickly, many borrowers also ask for **interim relief**, such as a stay on an auction or on handing over possession, while the case is pending.

## What is interim relief?

Interim relief is a temporary order passed while the main application is being heard. In a Section 17 case, it might ask the Tribunal to restrain the sale of the property, stop a transfer, or hold the status quo. It is not automatic. The Tribunal considers the facts, the documents and the stage of the case.

## Why the Tribunal may impose conditions

The Tribunal can, in its discretion, attach conditions to an interim order. A common condition is a **deposit or payment** towards the dues, which protects the bank's position while the borrower's challenge is heard. The amount and manner depend on the facts of each case, and there is no fixed formula that can be assumed. Read the current text of the Act and the Tribunal's orders for what applies in your case.

## Do not confuse this with Section 18

The pre-deposit for an **appeal to the DRAT** under Section 18 is a statutory requirement with its own rules. The post on Section 18 on this blog explains it. A condition in interim relief at the Section 17 stage is a different matter and is ordered by the Tribunal.

## What helps a borrower seek interim relief

1. A **clear, well-documented challenge** to the bank's measure.
2. **Prompt filing**, within 45 days of the measure, as the Act provides.
3. **Copies of notices**, statements of account and security documents.
4. A **proposal to deposit** a reasonable amount, where the borrower is able, which may show good faith.
5. **Accurate facts**, because the Tribunal will examine the borrower's own statements.

## Courts discourage shortcuts

The Supreme Court in *United Bank of India v. Satyawati Tondon* (2010) 8 SCC 110 discouraged writ petitions against SARFAESI action where the statutory remedy exists. A borrower should expect the Tribunal route to be the main path. The Supreme Court in *Mathew Varghese v. M. Amritha Kumar* (2014) 5 SCC 610 stressed that Rules 8 and 9 must be complied with, which borrowers often cite when examining a sale.

## Practical tips

- Do not wait for the auction date to move the Tribunal.
- Be ready to say what you can pay and when.
- Attend every hearing or ensure your advocate does.
- Keep checking the case status and any orders passed.

## Getting advice

Interim relief depends heavily on the facts and on the Tribunal's assessment. A prompt review of the papers is the best first step.
