Whether a guarantor stays liable after the borrower settles depends on what the settlement actually says. In a guarantor settlement borrower situation, the safest reading is that the guarantee ends only when the bank's debt is discharged in full, and a written settlement that records this is the best evidence of it.

## Why the guarantor's position is tied to the borrower's debt

A guarantor's liability is generally co-extensive with that of the principal borrower under Section 128 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, unless the guarantee provides otherwise. The guarantee exists to secure the same debt.

So if the borrower's debt is fully paid and closed, there is nothing left for the guarantee to secure. If only part of the debt is settled, or the bank reserves its rights, the position is less clear.

## When a settlement may release the guarantor

Section 134 of the Contract Act says a surety is discharged by a contract between the creditor and the principal debtor by which the principal debtor is released. Whether a particular settlement amounts to such a release depends on its wording and on the guarantee deed.

Points that usually matter:

- Whether the settlement letter says the entire dues are settled in full and final satisfaction.
- Whether the bank has reserved any right against the guarantor or the securities.
- Whether the borrower has paid every instalment of the settlement amount on time.
- Whether the guarantee deed contains terms that continue the liability despite a compromise with the borrower.

Do not assume release from the borrower's word alone. Ask for the papers.

## What if the bank still pursues the guarantor?

A bank that treats the settlement as incomplete may continue enforcement against the guarantor or a mortgaged property. A guarantor who has received a Section 13(2) notice can object under Section 13(3A), and if the bank rejects the objection it must give reasons (Mardia Chemicals v. UOI (2004) 4 SCC 311).

If a Section 13(4) measure follows, a person aggrieved may consider an application before the Debts Recovery Tribunal under Section 17. Check the current text of the Act for the period. ## What to collect after a settlement

Ask the borrower and the bank for copies of:

1. The signed settlement letter and the bank's acceptance.
2. Receipts for every payment made under the settlement.
3. The no-dues certificate and confirmation that the account is closed.
4. A release of any guarantee and return of security documents, including title deeds if you mortgaged property.

Our post on [the no-dues certificate and return of title documents](/blog/2026-12-09-settlement-no-dues-title-documents) lists what to look for.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a one-time settlement by the borrower end my guarantee?
Not automatically. If the settlement discharges the whole debt and the bank confirms this in writing, there is nothing left to guarantee. If the bank reserves rights against guarantors, the guarantee may continue.

### Can I ask the bank for a written release as guarantor?
Yes. You can ask the bank to confirm in writing that the account is closed and that the guarantee stands released. Keep a copy of the request and any reply.

### What if the borrower settles but defaults on the settlement instalments?
The bank may treat the settlement as lapsed and revive its claim for the original dues, depending on the settlement terms. Read the letter for what happens on default.
