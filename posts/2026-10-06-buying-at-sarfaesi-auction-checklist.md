title: Buying property at a SARFAESI auction: a due-diligence checklist

A bank auction can offer property at an attractive price, but the purchaser generally buys on an **"as is where is"** and often **"as is what is"** basis. Careful diligence matters.

## Before you bid

1. **Read the sale notice fully.** Check the reserve price, EMD, inspection dates, last date for bids, and the statement of known encumbrances.
2. **Verify the chain of title.** Obtain the title deeds, encumbrance certificate, mutation and tax receipts. The bank does not guarantee title.
3. **Confirm the bank's authority.** The notice must be issued by an authorised officer and the security interest should be created on the very property being sold.
4. **Check for litigation.** Look for pending Section 17 applications, appeals, writ petitions or civil suits, and any interim orders restraining sale.
5. **Physical inspection.** Confirm who is in possession, whether tenants or occupants exist, and whether the building matches sanctioned plans.
6. **Dues and approvals.** Ask about property tax, maintenance dues, society or RERA registrations and the transfer conditions, for example NOC requirements.

## After you win

- Pay the twenty-five per cent deposit on acceptance and the balance on time. Failure can lead to forfeiture and resale.
- Receive the **sale confirmation and certificate**, pay stamp duty and register it.
- If the property is not vacant, consider whether possession was already taken under Section 14 or symbolic possession only.

## Protecting a successful bid

A purchaser has standing to oppose challenges to the sale. Once the sale is confirmed and the price paid, courts tend to protect the purchaser unless there was fraud or serious illegality. Keep every receipt and communication.
