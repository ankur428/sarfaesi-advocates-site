If your loan has been sold by the bank, the next notice may come from an **asset reconstruction company (ARC)**. This often surprises borrowers, but the SARFAESI Act 2002 specifically provides for ARCs and their role.

## What is an ARC?

An ARC is a company registered with the Reserve Bank of India to acquire financial assets, typically non-performing loans, from banks and financial institutions. The Act allows the transfer of a financial asset to an ARC, which then steps into the position of the lender for the purposes of recovery.

## What powers does an ARC have?

An ARC that has acquired a loan can generally enforce the security interest under the SARFAESI Act, as a secured creditor, and may also pursue other recovery methods that the Act and other laws allow. The exact list of its powers is in the Act, so **check the current text of the Act**.

## What happens to the borrower's obligation?

The debt does not disappear. The borrower's liability continues, but the creditor changes. The borrower may now deal with the ARC for repayment, settlement or a restructuring proposal.

## What to check when an ARC sends a notice

1. **Proof of assignment:** ask for a copy of the assignment or transfer agreement, or at least a clear confirmation of the transfer.
2. **Notice of assignment:** check whether you were informed about the sale of the loan.
3. **The amount claimed:** ask for a statement of account showing the dues at the time of transfer and after.
4. **Security documents:** check that the ARC claims over the same property that was mortgaged to the original lender.
5. **Authority:** confirm that the person signing the notice is authorised.

## Remedies and settlements

If a borrower disagrees with an ARC's action, the usual remedy is a **Section 17 application** before the Debts Recovery Tribunal within the time limit in the Act. The Supreme Court in *Mardia Chemicals v. Union of India* (2004) 4 SCC 311 recognised that a borrower can object and that the creditor must reply with reasons. ARCs sometimes accept settlements, and the post on one-time settlements on this blog explains how to approach them.

Lessees and occupants may also have rights, as the Supreme Court discussed in *Harshad Govardhan Sondagar v. International Asset Reconstruction Co.* (2014) 6 SCC 1.

## Getting advice

An ARC case often involves old loan documents and several parties. A prompt review of the papers makes it easier to respond within time.
