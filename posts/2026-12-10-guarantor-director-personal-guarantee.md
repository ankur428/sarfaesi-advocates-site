A personal guarantee given by a director for a company loan can lead to SARFAESI action against the director personally. The Act treats a guarantor as a "borrower", so the director can receive a Section 13(2) notice and the assets charged by the guarantee can be enforced. This article explains what the exposure usually looks like under a personal guarantee SARFAESI director situation.

## Can a bank act against a director who gave a personal guarantee?

Yes, if the guarantee is valid and the loan has become a non-performing asset. The Act's definition of "borrower" includes a person who has given a guarantee as security for financial assistance granted to someone else. A director is not protected merely because the loan was taken by the company.

Under Section 128 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, a surety's liability is co-extensive with that of the principal debtor unless the contract says otherwise. The guarantee document therefore decides how far the director's liability goes.

## What does SARFAESI reach?

SARFAESI measures under Section 13(4) operate on secured assets, meaning assets over which a security interest was created. This matters in two ways:

- If the director also mortgaged or charged personal property to secure the loan, that property can be enforced against.
- If the guarantee is unsecured, the bank generally has to use another route, such as a recovery application before the DRT or a civil suit, to reach the director's other assets.

Check the guarantee deed and the security documents to see which applies. Our page on [guarantors and SARFAESI enforcement](/guarantors-sarfaesi-enforcement) covers the general position.

## What should the director do on receiving a notice?

The sixty-day period in Section 13(2) applies to the director as it does to the company. A director who receives a notice should:

1. Read what the notice says about the guarantor's own liability and the assets to be enforced.
2. Collect the guarantee deed, sanction letter, loan agreement and any later amendments.
3. Consider a written objection under Section 13(3A), to which the bank must reply with reasons if it does not accept the objection (Mardia Chemicals v. UOI (2004) 4 SCC 311).

Our post on [guarantors and SARFAESI: liability and defences](/blog/2026-10-04-guarantors-sarfaesi-liability-defences) lists points a guarantor can examine, including whether the loan terms were varied without consent. The post on [joint borrowers and who gets the notice](/blog/2026-11-12-section-13-2-notice-joint-borrowers) is also useful where several persons are named.

## What remedies does a director have?

A guarantor aggrieved by a measure under Section 13(4) may apply to the Debts Recovery Tribunal under Section 17, within the time the Act provides. Check the current text of the Act for the period. Writ petitions against SARFAESI action are discouraged where an effective remedy exists (United Bank of India v. Satyawati Tondon (2010) 8 SCC 110).

Where the company has also entered a settlement or insolvency process, how that affects the guarantee depends on its terms and the facts, so read the documents carefully.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does being a director make me liable for the company's loan?
Not by that fact alone. Liability arises from the guarantee you signed, or from security you created. Read the guarantee deed to see what you agreed to.

### Can the bank sell my house if I only signed a personal guarantee?
SARFAESI enforcement works against assets that were charged. If your house was not mortgaged, the bank generally has to use another recovery route to reach it.

### Can a director challenge the SARFAESI action before the DRT?
A guarantor who is aggrieved by a Section 13(4) measure can apply under Section 17. The grounds depend on the facts and the documents.
