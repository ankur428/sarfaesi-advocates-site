Most discussion of SARFAESI focuses on possession and auction of property. The Act also lists another measure a secured creditor may take after the notice period: taking over the management of the business of the borrower. This sits among the measures in Section 13(4), and the related procedure is in the Act's later provisions. Always read the current text of the Act, as the details have been amended over time.

## What the measure is

Under Section 13(4), if the borrower does not discharge the liability within the notice period under Section 13(2), the secured creditor may choose among the listed measures. One of them is to take over the management of the business of the borrower, with the right to transfer by lease, assignment or sale for realising the secured asset. This is different from simply taking possession of a building or a plot.

## Where it is relevant

The measure is generally relevant where the secured asset is tied to a running business, such as a factory or an enterprise whose value depends on its operations continuing. It is not a routine step for every account.

## What a borrower can check

- Whether a valid notice under Section 13(2) was issued and the notice period has actually run.
- Whether the bank replied with reasons to any written objection. In *Mardia Chemicals v. Union of India* (2004) 4 SCC 311, the Supreme Court recognised that a borrower can raise objections and that the creditor must respond with reasons.
- Whether the business and assets taken over are the ones that are actually secured to that creditor.
- Whether the Act applies to the asset at all, since some categories are excluded under Section 31.

## Remedies

A person aggrieved by a measure under Section 13(4) may consider an application under Section 17 before the Debts Recovery Tribunal, within the time limit in the Act. The Tribunal can examine whether the measure was taken in accordance with the Act and the Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules, 2002.

Writ petitions are not usually the first choice. In *United Bank of India v. Satyawati Tondon* (2010) 8 SCC 110, the Supreme Court discouraged writs where an effective statutory remedy exists.

## Practical steps

- Keep every notice, reply and proof of delivery in one file.
- List the secured assets and compare them with what the bank has actually taken over.
- Keep the business accounts, statutory records and employee details organised, since they may be needed before the Tribunal.
- Seek advice early, because time limits are short.

## In short

Takeover of management is one of several measures open to a secured creditor after the notice period. Its use depends on the facts and on strict compliance with the Act and the Rules, and a borrower should examine each step carefully before deciding on a response.
